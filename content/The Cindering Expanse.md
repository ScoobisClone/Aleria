#plane

Written by: Osric the Vedalken
# The Branch of Wrath
> _“There is no peace here. All who enter are to suffer yet another blow.”_

The **Cindering Expanse** is the [[Nehvarra|Branch]] of Wrath, a realm where anger is the fundamental law of existence. It exists as a vast and seemingly endless battlefield beneath a sky perpetually choked by a black ash. Fire falls from the sky like rain, the earth cracks and gives way to violence, and the distant horizon is forever illuminated by the flashes of war.

There is never any true beginning to a battle within this place, nor is there to be any true conclusion.

Armies march across fields that have been fought over for eternity. Warriors rise once more from ash with weapons already in hand, their minds filled with grievances they no longer remember. Entire fortresses are constructed with the sole purpose of being conquered. Even mountains are formed from the accumulated debris of these perpetual wars, only to be reduced back into nothing more than rubble.

Yet, the realm does not ask its inhabitants to fight.

It makes them want to.

Every soul within this horrible expanse is constantly confronted by the things that angered them in life.

The longer one remains within the Cindering Expanse, the more difficult it becomes to distinguish between righteous anger, hatred, vengeance, and violence for its own sake.

Eventually, the distinction ceases to matter.

There is only wrath.

---

# Nature of the Cindering Expanse
The Cindering Expanse is not geographically consistent in any way I could find.

Its terrain changes according to the intensity and nature of the anger present within it. A battlefield where thousands of souls rage against one another may stretch for hundreds of miles before abruptly collapsing into a narrow canyon where two individuals endlessly reenact a personal feud.

Furthermore, the Branch possesses no true horizon. Smoke, ash, and flame obscure the distance, creating the sensation that the world continues forever in every direction.

The ground is generally composed of blackened stone, volcanic glass, mineral-rich soil, and the remains of weapons. Beneath the surface lies an enormous network of molten channels through which [Inferis](Inferis) flows like blood through veins.

The land itself is hot, but even this heat is bearable compared to what makes the Cindering Expanse dangerous.

The true danger comes from agitation.

Those who enter the Branch often find themselves becoming increasingly irritable. Small inconveniences become intolerable. Minor disagreements become personal insults. Personal insults become grievances. Grievances become reasons to kill.

Now, this does not mean the Branch magically forces anger upon all who reside here. Rather, the Branch amplifies what is already present within the soul. Even a peaceful individual such as I can become irritated almost immediately.

I should clarify that last statement.

I was peaceful for approximately three hours.

Then a demon stole my notebook.

I was considerably less peaceful afterward.

A [Branded](Branded) who already belongs to Wrath may eventually find themselves incapable of experiencing anything without interpreting it as provocation. Several of the Branded I have spoken to insist that this is the greatest danger of the branch. The demons, naturally, disagree.

The demons say the greatest danger is the demons.

I am inclined to believe them.

---

# The Ashen Sky
I have observed the sky of the Cindering Expanse to be an enormous storm of black clouds, smoke, and burning ash.

There are no stars, no sun, nor a moon. Instead, the sky is periodically illuminated by enormous flashes of red and orange as storms of Inferis discharge through the clouds. These flashes are often accompanied by explosions so distant that their sound arrives minutes after the light.

Ash falls constantly.

In some regions, it falls like snow.

In others, it descends as burning fragments capable of cutting flesh.

There are particularly violent periods where the sky itself appears to bleed fire.

These phenomena I believe to correspond to great surges of wrath within [Materia](Materia). Wars, massacres, revolutions, genocides, and other such acts seem to all cause the skies of the Cindering Expanse to become more violent.

The inhabitants of the branch watch the mortal world closely.

This was something I initially found surprising.

I had expected the inhabitants of Nehvarra to be concerned primarily with themselves. Instead, I have found that many of the branded remain strangely interested in Materia.

They listen for wars.

They ask travelers about political disputes.

They argue over which nations are presently fighting.

Some even celebrate when a new conflict begins.

I asked one branded why.

He looked at me as though I had asked why a starving man was interested in food.

“Because it means we're still winning,” he told me.

I asked who _we_ were.

He laughed.

I have since decided not to ask him.

# The Fields of Cinders
The largest regions of the Cindering Expanse are known as the **Fields of Cinders** amongst the Branded down here.

These enormous plains are littered with the remains of countless conflicts.

Swords jut from the ground like grass, broken spears form fields, rusting armor lies half-buried beneath ash, and siege engines slowly burn without ever being completely consumed.

Entire armies can sometimes be found wandering through the fields, still fighting a war whose cause has long since been forgotten in their minds.

I have noticed that the branded rarely use the names assigned to the various regions by scholars of Materia. “Fields of Cinders” is a name I have only heard from outsiders and recently arrived souls. The older inhabitants simply call it _the Fields_.

When I asked why, my conversation partner became visibly irritated and told me that “there is only one field worth naming.”

I asked which one.

He punched me.

The Fields are perhaps the most culturally important region of the branch.

They are much more than a battlefield.

They are a meeting place.

Armies gather here. Mercenaries recruit here. Branded settle disputes here. Demons hunt here. Nobles hold demonstrations of strength here. There are even markets, although I use the word _market_ generously.

I saw one demon selling swords.

I asked where he had acquired them.

He pointed at the ground.

I looked down.

There were approximately four thousand swords sticking out of it.

“Fair enough,” I said.

He charged me three coins for one.

I suspect I was overcharged.

# The Culture of the Branded
The branded of wrath have developed a culture that I initially found difficult to understand.

Violence is not universally considered hostile.

This distinction is important.

A punch does not necessarily mean someone wants you dead.

A challenge to a duel does not necessarily mean someone hates you.

An insult may actually be an invitation to conversation.

A threat can, in some circumstances, be a greeting.

I spent nearly two weeks attempting to understand these distinctions.

I am still not certain I do.

The most consistent pattern I have discovered is that intention matters greatly.

A person who strikes another in genuine anger may be treated very differently from someone who strikes another as a test of strength. Likewise, surrendering during a duel may be regarded as cowardice by some and wisdom by others.

There is no universal code.

This surprised me.

I had expected Wrath to produce a society where violence followed rigid rules of strength. Instead, it appears to produce a society where everyone has their own understanding of when violence is justified.

Which, when I think about it, is considerably more appropriate.

# On Conversation
I have spoken with more branded than I can reasonably name.

Soldiers.

Kings.

Murderers.

Revolutionaries.

Executioners.

Victims.

People who insist they did nothing wrong.

People who insist they did everything wrong.

People who remember their deaths vividly.

People who remember almost nothing.

One woman named [[Sereth]] spent six hours explaining to me why she had been justified in killing her husband.

I asked her why.

She explained.

I asked whether she regretted it.

She said no.

I asked whether she would do it again.

She said yes.

I asked whether she had ever considered forgiving him.

She stared at me for approximately thirty seconds.

Then she asked me whether I wanted to leave.

I said yes.

We parted ways.

I have come to understand that the branded are not simply souls being punished.

They are people.

This may sound obvious.

It was not obvious to me when I arrived.

Many still retain their personalities, their memories, their prejudices, their loves, their hatreds, and their regrets. Some have changed enormously since death. Others seem almost exactly as they were in life.

---

# The Armies
The Fields contain armies of every conceivable kind.

Some are organized by mortal nationality, continuing wars that ended centuries ago.

Others are organized around causes.

Some fight for particular nobles.

Others fight simply because fighting is the only thing they remember how to do.

I encountered an army consisting almost entirely of branded who claimed to have died in the same war.

They had been fighting one another for so long that none of them could remember which side they had originally belonged to.

When I asked their commander whether this troubled him, he said:

“No.”

I asked why.

He replied:

“Because if I knew, I'd have to choose.”

I found this answer deeply unsettling.

I wrote it down.

He then demanded payment for the quote.

I paid him.

---

# The Grievance Roads

The Cindering Expanse contains a network of paths known amongst the branded as the Grievance Roads.

Calling them roads is generous.

Some are little more than trails through ash.

Others are enormous black highways stretching across the Branch.

Their destination, however, is what makes them unusual.

The Grievance Roads lead to conflicts.

A soul traveling one may suddenly find themselves standing before the person who wronged them in life.

Another may arrive at the site of an ancient betrayal.

Another may find themselves surrounded by an army representing everything they once hated.

The roads appear to respond to the traveler.

The greater the grievance, the stronger the road becomes.

I was told this by a branded named [[Hesk]], who claims to have walked one for forty-three years.

I asked where it took him.

“To him.”

I asked who _him_ was.

Hesk refused to answer.

I asked again.

He threw a stone at me.

I have begun to suspect that asking the same question twice is considered provocative here.

---

# The Mountains of Ruin
Across the Cindering Expanse stand enormous mountains composed of stone, weapons, armor, and the remains of destroyed fortifications.

They are known as the Mountains of Ruin.

I have never seen anything quite like them.

At a distance, they resemble ordinary mountains.

Up close, they are anything but.

Entire swords protrude from their slopes.

Old shields form layers in the rock.

There are fortifications buried inside them.

I found a helmet embedded approximately eighty feet above the ground.

I was told by three separate branded that it belonged to a famous general.

I was told by another that it belonged to a woman who had never held a sword.

I was told by a demon that it was his.

I have concluded that the helmet is probably not worth further investigation.

The mountains are not permanent.

When enough rage accumulates around them, they begin to tremble.

Eventually, they collapse.

The resulting avalanches of stone and weapons can destroy entire armies, after which the survivors immediately begin fighting over the ruins.

I asked why.

Nobody seemed to understand the question.

---

# The Iron Tempests
The deepest regions of the Cindering Expanse are plagued by enormous storms known as Iron Tempests.

These storms do not carry rain.

They carry weapons.

Blades, arrows, axes, chains, spears, fragments of siege weapons, and pieces of shattered armor are hurled through the air at tremendous speeds.

The weapons do not seem to originate from anywhere.

They simply appear.

The longer a battle lasts beneath an Iron Tempest, the more violent the storm becomes.

Some demons deliberately wage wars beneath them, believing that surviving an Iron Tempest proves one's worth.

I have not found a single branded who agrees.

This has not stopped the demons.

The survivors of these storms are often covered in fragments of weapons that have become permanently embedded into their bodies.

Such demons are sometimes called "Storm-Born".

I encountered one.

I asked him whether the name referred to his origin or his appearance.

He said yes.

I asked him to elaborate.

He threw an axe at me.

There is apparently a pattern developing here.

---

# The Law of Escalation
If there is one principle I believe governs the Cindering Expanse above all others, it is this:

**Every conflict must become worse.**

An argument becomes a confrontation.

A confrontation becomes violence.

Violence becomes bloodshed.

Bloodshed becomes war.

War becomes annihilation.

The branch itself appears to resist attempts to de-escalate conflict.

A warrior who throws down their weapon may find another suddenly placed in their hand.

A person attempting to apologize may hear their own memories whispering reasons why they were justified.

A retreating army may discover that the landscape has shifted to place its enemies directly in front of them.

Yet I have not determined whether the branch actually prevents peace.

This distinction has become one of my primary subjects of study.

Several branded insist that peace is impossible.

Several others insist that peace is merely difficult.

One particularly old branded told me that peace is “the hardest form of victory.”

I asked him whether he had ever achieved it.

He laughed.

Then he began crying.

I did not ask another question.

The Cindering Expanse does not necessarily force its inhabitants to become monsters.

It makes becoming a monster easier.

I have concluded this to be worse.

---

# The Demons of Wrath
The native demons of the Cindering Expanse are not branded.

This distinction is important enough that I am writing it down twice.

Demons are born from sin.

The branded are sinners who have come to reside within Nehvarra.

The demons of wrath therefore have never lived mortal lives.

They were born from anger itself.

Their forms vary considerably.

Some resemble armored warriors.

Others are living flames, beasts covered in weapons, enormous humanoids made from molten iron, or barely recognizable masses of teeth, claws, and fists.

Their appearance often reflects the particular form of Wrath from which they emerged.

A demon born from battlefield fury may resemble a soldier.

One born from personal vengeance may appear almost human.

One born from genocidal hatred may be large enough to resemble a living siege engine.

I asked several demons whether they remembered being born.

Most laughed.

One told me:

“Do you remember learning to be angry?”

I said yes.

He said:

“Then you remember being born.”

I wrote this down.

I think it may be important.

---

# Demonic Society
The demons of wrath appear to possess their own culture entirely separate from that of the branded.

They do not consider themselves damned.

They do not generally consider themselves prisoners.

Many appear to regard the Cindering Expanse as their natural home.

This is perhaps obvious.

I nevertheless found myself asking a demon whether he wanted to leave.

He looked at me with genuine confusion.

“Why?”

I explained that there were other places in Nehvarra.

He asked whether they had wrath.

I said no.

He laughed.

“Then why would I go?”

I have since become much more careful about assuming that the inhabitants of Nehvarra desire escape.

The branded may dream of leaving.

The demons were born here.

For many of them, there is nowhere else they would rather be.

---

# The Infernal Nobility of Wrath
The hierarchy of the [Infernal Nobility](Infernal%20Nobility) is particularly visible within the Cindering Expanse.

Power is respected here.

Not universally.

But consistently enough that I have begun to suspect that violence and social status are deeply intertwined.

Viscounts command fortresses.

Counts control regions of the Fields.

Marquises frequently control territory bordering others.

Dukes command armies and maintain enormous war camps.

Princes are treated with a mixture of fear and admiration.

Kings are something else entirely.

I have seen a King of Wrath enter a battlefield.

Every combatant stopped.

They recognized something stronger than themselves.

The King did not need to threaten anyone.

His existence was the threat.

The branded understand this better than anyone.

Many aspire to climb the hierarchy.

Some believe that strength is the only meaningful measure of worth.

Others despise this philosophy and believe the nobility has simply found a sophisticated way to justify the same violence that damned them.

I asked one duke which side was correct.

He told me:

“Both.”

I asked how.

He told me:

“That's why we're still here.”

---

# On Challenges
There is apparently a formal concept of challenge among the higher ranks of the branch.

A challenge is apparently more than an invitation to fight.

It is a declaration that one's position, authority, or worth is being questioned.

Refusing one can have serious social consequences.

Accepting one can have considerably more serious physical consequences.

I have been told that challenges between branded are generally expected to have agreed-upon conditions.

No interference.

No outside assistance.

A defined battlefield.

Sometimes a surrender is permitted.

Sometimes it is not.

These customs vary considerably depending upon the participants.

I have also been informed that challenging a noble without understanding the rules is “a good way to become a corpse.”

I pointed out that everyone here was already dead.

My conversation partner stared at me.

I apologized.

---

# The Throne of Cinders
At the center of the Cindering Expanse lies the domain of its Lord.

The Throne of Cinders is not a palace in any conventional sense.

It is a colossal fortress built into the remains of a shattered mountain.

The fortress is constantly under siege.

Armies of demons and branded assault its walls at nearly every hour of every day.

Some claim to be rebels.

Others seek glory.

Some simply want to test themselves.

Many want to kill the Lord of Wrath and claim his place.

None have succeeded.

The battles around the fortress are so enormous that I have been told they have become a permanent part of the branch itself.

I asked whether [[Rhazgor]] actually ordered these assaults.

The answer was:

“No.”

I asked whether he permits them.

The answer was:

“Yes.”

I asked why.

The branded I was speaking to shrugged.

“He likes watching.”

This answer came from six different people.

I have therefore tentatively accepted it.

# Rhazgor, the Howling Pyre

>Lord of Wrath
>The War-Wrought Titan
>The Howling Pyre
>The Furnace

At the center of the Cindering Expanse stands **Rhazgor, the Howling Pyre**.

I have seen him.

This is perhaps the most important statement I have made in this document.

I have seen Rhazgor.

I would like this recorded because several colleagues in Materia informed me that I would not.

They were wrong.

I was nearly killed.

They were partially right.

Rhazgor is a colossal figure whose exact form is difficult to perceive.

He resembles a titan forged from blackened iron and volcanic stone, with enormous portions of his body glowing like the interior of a furnace. His armor appears to have been assembled from the weapons of countless wars, while chains and broken standards hang from his body like trophies.

Fire pours continuously from the cracks in what could be considered his flesh.

Where blood should flow, Inferis burns.

His face is concealed behind a massive iron war-mask, split down the center by a single vertical opening through which a furnace-like light can be seen.

His voice is not merely heard.

It is felt.

When Rhazgor speaks, the ground trembles.

When he screams, armies have been known to begin fighting without understanding why.

His howl is said to be capable of crossing the entirety of his branch.

I can confirm this.

I have heard it from approximately eighty miles away.

I do not recommend it.

---

# My Conversation with Rhazgor
I had intended to ask Rhazgor approximately thirty-seven questions.

I managed six.

The first was:

“What is wrath?”

He stared at me.

I was beginning to regret the question.

Then he answered:

“Joy.”

I wrote this down.

I asked what he meant.

He said:

“Every battle is like a play.”

I asked whether wrath was therefore the desire to make others suffer.

He laughed.

“No.”

I asked what I had gotten wrong.

He leaned closer.

“Wrath is the joy of combat for combat, not for suffering.”

I admit that I found this answer considerably more interesting than I had anticipated.

I asked what mortals were owed.

He said:

“Nothing.”

I asked why they became angry, then.

He then asked me whether I had ever been angry because I believed something had been taken from me.

I said yes.

He asked whether I had ever wanted the person responsible to suffer.

I said yes.

He asked whether I had ever believed that suffering would make the original wound disappear.

I did not answer.

He laughed.

I ended the interview shortly afterward.

Not because I was afraid.

I was very afraid.

There was also the matter of the floor beginning to melt.

# The Nature of Rhazgor
Rhazgor is no god of war.

He is wrath given will.

War is merely one expression of him.

His branch encompasses rage, vengeance, hatred, fury, bloodlust, indignation, violent retribution, and the intoxicating certainty that one's anger justifies one's actions.

Rhazgor does not necessarily hate his enemies.

Hatred requires recognizing another as something worthy of hatred.

Rhazgor is interested in something more fundamental:

The need to strike.

Rhazgor listens.

Rhazgor is almost everywhere.

---

# Rhazgor and the Branded
I expected Rhazgor to despise the branded.

He does not.

I expected him to love them.

He does not.

He appears to regard them with something closer to fascination.

The branded are proof that wrath has claimed mortal souls.

Every new branded who arrives is another indication that wrath still possesses power within Materia.

In a second interview, I asked Rhazgor whether he considered the branded his children.

He answered:

“No.”

I asked whether he considered them servants.

“No.”

I asked what they were, then.

He looked at me for a very long time.

“Witnesses.”

I found this answer deeply unpleasant.

I asked what they were witnesses to.

He sounded like he was smiling.

I did not ask another question.

---

# On Worship
Rhazgor does not appear to require worship.

This has caused considerable disagreement among the branded.

Some worship him.

Some despise him.

Some consider him simply another ruler.

Others believe that worshipping wrath is nonsensical because wrath does not require faith.

One branded told me:

“You don't worship fire.”

I asked why. After all, there *were* people who worshipped fire in Materia.

“Because you respect it.”

I asked whether that was how he regarded Rhazgor.

He laughed.

“No. I fear fire.”

---

# The Blood-Furnace
Deep beneath the Throne of Cinders lies what the Branded call the Blood-Furnace.

I have not personally entered it.

I would like to emphasize this before anyone accuses me of hypocrisy.

There are limits to academic dedication.

The Blood-Furnace is said to be the point where much of the Inferis generated by the Cindering Expanse converges.

The inhabitants describe it as an enormous chamber of molten stone and black flame.

Every act of wrath within the branch is believed to eventually feed into it.

More importantly, its intensity appears to correspond with acts of wrath occurring within Materia.

When wars erupt in the mortal world, the furnace burns brighter.

When massacres occur, it roars.

Rhazgor periodically descends into it.

When he emerges, the inhabitants claim he is stronger.

I asked whether this meant Rhazgor could ever truly die.

The answer I received was:

“Of course.”

I asked how.

The branded shrugged.

“Stop being angry.”

I laughed.

He did not.

I have since regretted laughing.

---

# Incursions into Materia
Rhazgor's influence can extend beyond Nehvarra.

When extraordinary quantities of wrath accumulate within Materia, the Cindering Expanse may begin to overlap with the mortal world.

These events are known as [[Infernal Incursions]].

The signs are often subtle at first.

Arguments become more frequent.

Old grievances resurface.

People begin remembering insults they had forgotten.

Weapons appear where none should be.

Ash falls from otherwise clear skies.

Animals become unusually aggressive.

Eventually, the landscape itself begins changing.

Stone blackens.

Rivers become hot.

Smoke appears on the horizon despite there being no fire.

The boundary between Materia and the Cindering Expanse becomes increasingly thin.

At the center of such an Incursion, Rhazgor may manifest.

I have never witnessed such an event personally.

I have, however, interviewed three survivors.

All three gave different accounts.

All three agreed on one detail.

They heard laughter before the fire began.

# The Philosophy of Wrath
After spending considerable time within the Cindering Expanse, I believe I have begun to understand what this branch actually represents.

Wrath is not simply rage.

It is not simply hatred.

It is not even violence.

It is anger given purpose.

More specifically, it is the exhilaration found within conflict—the joy of the fight, the rush of battle, and the satisfaction of overcoming something that refuses to yield.

Once the weapons are drawn, the reason often becomes secondary.

The fight itself becomes the reason.

Every soul here has something it wants to fight.

Every demon believes it was born to fight.

Every army believes its cause is righteous.

Every warrior seeks an opponent worthy of their strength.

And unlike many of the other branches, they do not necessarily wish for the conflict to end.

Because there is always another opponent.

Another challenge.

Another battle.

Another weapon waiting to be drawn.

The Cindering Expanse does not burns because someone still wants to fight.

And somewhere above it all stands Rhazgor, laughing through the flames.
