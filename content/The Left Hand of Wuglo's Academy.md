> **“How does a mortal impose their will upon Essentia?”**

This is the first question posed to every student who enters the Left Hand of Wuglo's Academy. 

Magic is not a singular discipline. Though all formal spell craft ultimately concerns to the manipulation of [[Mana]] ([[Essentias|Essentia]] by extension), the methods by which a practitioner imposes their will upon it can differ as greatly as the practitioners themselves. Where some construct intricate magical structures, others command through language. 

The Academy therefore recognizes five fundamental approaches to formal magic. Each shall be represented by one of the five fingers of the Left Hand as they are within the Academy. Note that each of these approaches are completely different philosophies of magic and not something like different collections of spells. 

**The Circle** — _Through structure._
**The Rune** — _Through language._
**The Glyph** — _Through expression._
**The Domain** — _Through understanding._
**The Innate** — _Through existence._

A student typically devotes themselves to one finger, though it is not uncommon for a student to dabble in another fundamental finger.  Few achieve mastery of more than one.
- Note: For the sake of understanding each of the fundamentals of magic will be accompanied by a real-world academic comparison.

# The Thumb - Circle Magic
`The Magic of Structure`
Of all the formal magical disciplines taught, Circle Magic is perhaps the most immediately recognizable as wizardry. The fundamental principle is simple:
> **Do the thinking before the magic begins.**

A Circle Mage constructs a self-contained magical structure capable of performing a predetermined sequence of operations. The **Circle** acts as a framework into which Essentia (most usually Mana) may be introduced, manipulated, redirected, and ultimately released.

As such, a novice might want to learn magic and believe it to be: "I want fire." Meanwhile, a Circle Mage would instead think: "Input Mana → establish thermal conversion → constrain vector → establish ignition condition → regulate output → terminate when condition X is met."

The distinction is fundamental as the Circle performs the majority of the calculations on the caster's behalf. Once properly constructed, even an enormously complicated magical operation can be executed without requiring the caster to consciously perform every individual step. Thus, Circle Magic is among the most reliable and reproducible forms of wizardry.

A properly designed Circle does not care whether its creator is having a good day. It does not become distracted, forget an instruction, or suddenly decide that perhaps the fireball should travel sideways instead. It simply performs the instructions it was given. This reliability is precisely why Circle Magic forms the foundation of the majority of magical research.

**Academic Analogy.** Programming
- A Circle Mage does not necessarily know more magic than another wizard, but they would certainly know how to write a better magical program.

## Strengths
- Extremely precise.
- Highly repeatable.
- Excellent for rituals and large-scale magical operations.
- Capable of automating complicated magical processes.
- Particularly useful for enchantment, alchemy, wards, teleportation, summoning, and other structured effects.
- Circles can be studied, modified, optimized, and reproduced by other practitioners.
## Weaknesses
- Most Circles require preparation.
- A suitable physical space or magical medium is often necessary.
- Errors in construction can result in catastrophic failures.
- Advanced Circles may require years of study before they can be understood.
- Improvisation is difficult without extensive knowledge of the underlying principles.

# The Index - Rune Magic
`The Magic of Language`
Rune Magic is founded upon the belief that Essentia can be influenced through meaning. **Runes** are the components of a magical language whose meanings possess semantic and metaphysical weight. By arranging them into appropriate sequences, a Rune Mage constructs statements that Essentia is compelled to interpret.

A simple command might be represented as:
> **FIRE** — **GO** — **FORWARD**

Rather than how a Circle Mage might construct an elaborate magical system for producing and directing flame, the Rune Mage instead tells the magic what must happen. This makes Rune Magic less concerned with calculations, as it is all about language. In which, its practitioners study magical grammar, syntax, semantics, etymology, context, and interpretation. The distinction between two seemingly synonymous Runes may be insignificant in ordinary writing yet produce radically different magical results.

Ancient civilizations possessed Runes whose meanings have since changed, fragmented, or disappeared entirely. Some surviving Runes are still used despite scholars possessing only incomplete understandings of their original meanings.

This has occasionally produced spectacular breakthroughs.

It has also produced spectacular explosions.

**Academic Analogy.** Linguistics and Law
- A Rune Mage compels and tells magic to do something. Interpretations may vary. 

## Strengths
- Highly flexible.
- Excellent for enchantments and permanent magical effects.
- Can produce powerful effects through concise commands.
- Particularly useful for communicating magical instructions to objects.
- Can interact with ancient magical artifacts whose functions depend upon forgotten magical languages.
- Does not generally require the enormous physical structures associated with complex Circle Magic.
## Weaknesses
- Ambiguity can produce unintended results.
- Translation errors can be dangerous.
- Context can dramatically alter the interpretation of a Rune.
- Ancient or Primordial Runes may possess meanings that mortal scholars cannot safely comprehend.
- Complex effects require increasingly sophisticated magical grammar.

Despite what a common person may believe, Rune Mages do not merely memorize random symbols. They learn to understand the fundamentals of the language of magic.

# The Middle - Glyph Magic
`The Magic of Expression`
If Circle Magic represents structure and Rune Magic represents language, Glyph Magic represents expression. Rather than relying on external constructs or systems, they Glyph Mage inscribes magical principles directly upon their [[Vessel Homepage|Vessel]]. 

A **Glyph** is a conceptual instruction embedded within the architecture of the caster's Vessel. Many basic Glyphs are universally recognized throughout the magical world. Among the most common are those representing concepts such as: 
- **Flame**
- **Ice**
- **Shape**
- **Motion**
- **Binding**
- **Severing**
- **Barrier**
A practitioner inscribed with a Flame Glyph might be able to produce or manipulate flame through their Vessel. A practitioner possessing a Motion Glyph might influence the movement of an object or their own Essentia. You get the idea. Now, while the possibilities are considerable, the limitations are equally important.

## The One-Glyph Principle
Most practitioners, while being able to hold multiple Glyphs, can maintain only one active Glyph at a time. The exact reason for this limitation remains a matter of scholarly debate. The prevailing theory holds that a Vessel must dedicate a portion of its internal architecture to interpreting and sustaining a Glyph. Attempting to maintain multiple independent Glyphs simultaneously places massive, contradictory demands upon that architecture.

For the average Glyph Mage, attempting to activate a second Glyph while the first remains active is therefore ineffective at best and dangerous at worst, so an example of action would be to use on Glyph, deactivate it, and then activate a second Glyph. 

## Glyph Fusion
Rather than maintaining several Glyphs independently, Glyph Mages learn to fuse Glyphs together into one. This fusion takes two or more recognized Glyphs and combines their principles into a new, unified Glyph. The resulting Glyph is no longer treated by the Vessel as several separate instructions. However, this also means that the resultant Glyph can easily become more limited in its scope, even if more powerful.

For example, a Flame Glyph and an Expansion Glyph might individually produce flame and expansion. A properly fused Flame-Expansion Glyph, however, could produce an explosive manifestation of flame as a single unified effect. Even if that distinction may appear trivial to an inexperience mage, it is not.

Creating useful fusions require the practitioner to understand how the principles represented by the component Glyphs interact. Some combinations are naturally compatible. Others are not. As such, a talented Glyph Mage may discover that two Glyphs can be fused into an elegant and highly efficient expression. Another combination may technically function but require considerably more effort and energy to achieve the same result. A poor fusion might even place excessive strain upon the Vessel.

Thus, the skill of a Glyph Mage is not measured by their number of Glyphs or even maybe the strength of their Glyphs but by how well they can combine them.

Furthermore, a very important matter to note is that the individual interpretation of the fusion matters as well. For example, two Glyph Mages may fuse together the same two Glyphs, but have different results based on their own intentions, alongside with their own efficiencies and abilities.

**Academic Analogy.** Art
- All that Glyph Mages do are compose and paint magical expressions onto themselves.

## Strengths
- Fast once the appropriate Glyph has been prepared.
- Highly adaptable.
- Particularly effective for combat and other situations requiring rapid magical responses.
- Allows practitioners to develop personalized magical techniques.
- Can imitate certain characteristics of innate magical abilities.
- Does not generally require external magical infrastructure.
- Glyph Fusion allows simple principles to produce increasingly sophisticated effects.

## Weaknesses
- Most practitioners can maintain only one active Glyph at a time.
- More complicated effects require increasingly sophisticated fusions.
- Poor fusions can waste considerable amounts of Essentia.
- Unstable fusions may damage the Vessel.
- Developing new fusions requires experimentation and considerable understanding of magical principles.
- A practitioner who loses access to an appropriate Glyph or fusion may be unable to reproduce an otherwise familiar effect.

# The Ring - Domain Magic
`The Magic of Reality`

The rarest of the five, a Domain Mage spreads their own Essentia throughout an area, forming a temporary **Domain** in which their Vessel imposes itself upon reality. Within this Domain, the caster possess vastly greater freedom to manipulate their respective Essentia and alter the behavior of magical phenomena. Rather than relying upon a pre-written Circle, the Domain Mage must continuously perform the calculations necessary to determine how their magic behaves within the space.

Every Domain has a fixed **Volume**. While its shape may be changed, its total Volume remains the same. A skilled Domain Mage may therefore reshape their Domain from a sphere into a narrow corridor, a wide plane, or another configuration without increasing the amount of space they control.

A Domain's **Density** determines how much of the caster's Essentia is concentrated within that Volume. Greater Density makes it easier to produce and maintain certain phenomena within the Domain, allowing the caster to exert greater influence over reality in a concentrated area. A Domain Mage with high Density may accomplish in one portion of their Domain what would require considerably more effort across a less concentrated one.

This can be likened to pushing a boulder. One person may struggle to move it, while several people pushing together can move it with considerably greater ease. Volume determines how much space a Domain encompasses; Density determines how much force the caster can bring to bear within that space.

Unfortunately, all of this makes Domain Magic perhaps the most computationally demanding discipline taught be the Left Hand. A beginner may only be able to maintain a small Domain for a few seconds, while experienced practitioners can maintain larger and more complex Domains for considerably longer.

**Academic Analogy.** Mathematics and Physics
- A Circle Mage uses a solved equation. A Domain Mage solves the equation while reality is happening. 

## Strengths
- Exceptional flexibility.
- Does not require pre-made mediums.
- Extraordinary battlefield control.
- Can dynamically reshape its area.
- Can concentrate Essentia to produce difficult phenomena.
- Can produce effects impractical or impossible to prepare within a conventional Circle.

## Weaknesses
- Enormous cognitive burden.
- Requires significant concentration.
- Limited by the caster's Volume.
- Increasing Density demands greater Essentia expenditure.
- Extremely difficult to maintain while multitasking.
- Excessive complexity can overwhelm the caster's Vessel.

# The Pinky - Innate Magic
`The Magic of the Self`

The fifth finger of the Left Hand is unusual. Unlike the other four, Innate Magic is not truly a discipline that can be taught systematically. The Academy nevertheless recognizes it because its existence is too significant to ignore. Innate Magic describes supernatural abilities that originate not from a learned magical methodology, but from the individual themselves. 

Sorcery, certain bloodline techniques, and other unusual forms of personal magical expression may fall under this classification. As is with all magic, the matter of importance here is the origin of the effect. An innate practitioner possesses something within themselves that makes the phenomenon possible in the first place.

However, this does not necessarily mean that Innate Magic is simple. An innate practitioner may spend years refining, studying, and mastering their abilities. What cannot be taught is the fundamental trait that allows those abilities to exist. Regardless, the Academy can still guide these practitioners into bettering their capabilities.

**Academic Analogy.** None

## Strengths
- Highly individualized.
- Can produce highly specialized effects.
- Does not necessarily require formal magical methodology.
- Can develop alongside the practitioner.
- Can produce effects difficult or impossible to replicate through conventional magic.
- Has the lowest learning curve of the five.

## Weaknesses
- Difficult to reproduce or teach in a group setting.
- Dependent on the individual's innate qualities.
- Difficult to systematically improve.
- May have limited applications.
- Can be difficult for the Academy to study or categorize.
