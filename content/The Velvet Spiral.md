# The Branch of Lust
## The Velvet Spiral
> _“Desire is not satisfied by possession. It is satisfied only long enough to discover something else to desire.”_

Of all the Branches I have visited thus far, the Velvet Spiral is perhaps the most difficult to describe without sounding either prudish or embarrassingly sentimental.

I shall attempt to do neither.

Lust is desire.

Not merely the desire for flesh, though there is certainly an abundance of that here. It is the desire for intimacy, affection, pleasure, beauty, companionship, creation, and ultimately the desire to extend oneself into something beyond oneself.

The body is merely one method.

A child is another.

So is a painting.

A song.

A philosophy.

A religion.

A lover who remembers you.

The Velvet Spiral concerns the hunger to experience these things and, more importantly, the hunger to leave something of yourself behind through them.

---

## First Impressions
The Velvet Spiral resembles an enormous palace.

I say _resembles_ because calling it a palace implies that it was constructed according to some coherent architectural principle.

It was not.

Corridors twist around one another without apparent purpose. Staircases lead into bedrooms, bedrooms open into gardens, and gardens occasionally open into places that I am reasonably certain were not physically located inside this "building".

The walls are covered in velvet.

The floors are covered in velvet.

Several inhabitants are covered in velvet.

I was informed that this last practice is optional.

The palace is beautiful, naturally.

The Velvet Spiral wants to be inviting.

It wants you to enter.

And, most importantly, it wants you to want something.

---

## The Nature of Lust
Like the other Branches, this one does not create desire.

It amplifies what is already there.

A lonely person may find themselves surrounded by affection.

A lustful person may find endless lovers.

An artist may discover an audience willing to adore their work.

A scholar may find disciples eager to preserve their teachings.

A parent may see their children grow into living continuations of themselves.

The temptation is rarely something completely alien.

It is usually something the victim already wanted.

As always, that is what makes it effective.

Why would it ever ask, _“What can I make you desire?”_

It is easier to ask, _“What do you already desire?”_

Then it gives you more.

---

## The Rooms of Wanting
The palace is divided into no formal districts, though the inhabitants have given names to many of its more persistent locations.

The **Chambers of Adoration** are perhaps the most popular.

They are enormous halls where inhabitants gather with lovers, admirers, friends, and strangers, each offering affection in whatever form the other desires. Some come seeking romance. Others seek companionship. Others simply wish to feel wanted by another person.

The danger is that the Chambers always seem capable of providing _exactly_ the connection one desires.

I spoke with a woman who had spent several decades within one such chamber, surrounded by people who adored her.

I asked her why she remained.

She answered, quite simply:

_“Because they leave when I ask them to.”_

I asked what she meant.

She smiled.

_“I want them to stay.”_

I believe she had been there for some time.

The **Gardens of Union** are considerably less subtle.

They are beautiful places of flowers, fountains, music, and warm evening light, where lovers meet, reunite, and occasionally remain for decades without noticing the passage of time.

Some relationships appear genuine.

This is important.

Not everything within the Velvet Spiral is false.

Some inhabitants have found genuine affection here.

Some have formed friendships.

Some have fallen in love.

The horror is that the Branch can make something genuine into something insatiable.

One person is enough.

Until they are not.

One embrace is enough.

Until another is wanted.

One night is enough.

Until the next must be better.

The Velvet Spiral is exceptionally patient.

It never needs to convince someone that what they have is worthless.

It merely convinces them that something more might be better.

---

## The Propagation
The deeper reaches of the Spiral become increasingly strange.

Here, desire ceases to concern only what one wishes to _receive_ and begins to concern what one wishes to leave behind.

These areas are dominated by creators.

Artists paint walls that paint themselves.

Musicians perform songs which cause their listeners to hum the melodies long after leaving.

Writers produce books that seem to contain additional pages whenever someone becomes interested in them.

Teachers establish schools.

Prophets establish cults.

Inventors create machines designed specifically to build more machines.

Every creation carries something of its creator.

That is the temptation.

The promise that perhaps you do not need to die completely.

Perhaps some part of you can continue.

Perhaps your name can survive.

Perhaps your thoughts can survive.

Perhaps your children can carry you forward.

Perhaps your followers can speak your words.

Perhaps your work can outlive your body.

The Spiral whispers that this is possible.

Then it asks:

**Why stop at one?**

One student can teach another.

One follower can recruit another.

One book can be copied.

One doctrine can be preached.

One child can have children.

One idea can become a movement.

And suddenly something that began as _you_ no longer requires you at all.

I believe this is one of the deepest forms of Lust.

Not merely wanting to possess the world.

Wanting the world to carry a piece of you.

---

## The Propagated
There are inhabitants who have devoted their entire existence to this principle.

I met one who had created a religion during his mortal life.

He had been dead for nearly four hundred years.

His religion had not.

In fact, it had become considerably larger.

When I asked him whether he was proud, he smiled.

_"Of course."_

I asked him whether the religion still followed his original teachings.

He hesitated.

_"Not exactly."_

I asked whether this bothered him.

_"At first."_

He laughed.

_"Then I realized that meant it was alive."_

This struck me as unexpectedly profound.

I asked him whether he still considered himself the religion's creator.

He answered:

_"No."_

He pointed toward a nearby procession of followers.

_"I am its ancestor."_

There was something deeply unsettling about the pride with which he said it.

---

## The Culture of Desire
The society of the Velvet Spiral is surprisingly cooperative.

This should not be mistaken for kindness.

Everyone here wants something.

Consequently, everyone understands that everyone else wants something.

Desire is treated almost like a social language.

Compliments are currency.

Attention is currency.

Beauty is currency.

Secrets are currency.

Affection is currency.

Influence is perhaps the most valuable currency of all.

The inhabitants constantly bargain with one another.

A person may offer companionship in exchange for loyalty.

A teacher may offer knowledge in exchange for disciples.

An artist may offer beauty in exchange for admiration.

A lover may offer affection in exchange for devotion.

A prophet may offer certainty in exchange for belief.

None of this is necessarily forced.

The Velvet Spiral rarely needs chains.

---

## The Carnal
It would be dishonest to describe the branch without addressing its most obvious expression.

The Velvet Spiral is extraordinarily sensual.

Music drifts through its halls.

Perfumes linger in the air.

Its inhabitants dress themselves according to whatever they find beautiful.

There are countless places dedicated to romance, physical pleasure, courtship, and intimacy.

The branch understands the body extremely well.

It knows how attraction begins.

It knows how anticipation works.

It knows the difference between affection and obsession.

More disturbingly, it knows how easily the two can become confused.

I spoke with a branded woman who had spent decades pursuing a particular lover.

When I asked why she had not simply left him, she looked genuinely confused.

_"Because I love him."_

I asked whether he loved her.

She smiled.

_"He will."_

I did not ask what she meant.

I have begun to recognize certain answers as warnings.

---

## The Spiral Itself
There is a peculiar consistency to the architecture.

The further one travels, the more circular the corridors become.

Eventually, one begins passing the same rooms again.

Except they are never quite the same.

I believe this is deliberate.

The Branch seems to understand that desire is cyclical.

Pleasure leads to attachment.

Attachment leads to creation.

Creation leads to propagation.

Propagation creates new relationships.

Those relationships create new desires.

And so the process begins again.

The Spiral does not even need to trap its inhabitants.

It simply needs to make sure they always have somewhere else they want to go.

---

## Savaen, the Silk-Tongued Maw
I eventually encountered [[Savaen]].

I use _encountered_ rather than _met_ because I never actually saw her.

I heard her voice.

It came from somewhere behind a curtain.

Then from a mirror.

Then from the woman standing beside me.

Then, briefly, from my own mouth.

She asked me what I wanted.

I told her that I wanted to understand the Velvet Spiral.

She laughed.

_"No."_

I asked what she meant.

_"That is what you tell yourself you want."_

There was a pause.

_"What do you actually want?"_

I declined to answer.

She seemed delighted.

_"Good."_

The conversation ended shortly afterward.

I have since become rather uncomfortable around mirrors.

---

## The Philosophy of Lust
After spending considerable time within the Velvet Spiral, I believe I understand its central principle.

Lust is not simply the desire for pleasure.

It is the desire to experience, to possess, to connect, and to continue.

It is the hunger for another body.

Another embrace.

Another lover.

Another child.

Another admirer.

Another creation.

Another disciple.

Another person who carries something of you.

I suppose this is what makes Lust different from Gluttony.

Gluttony wishes to consume the world.

Lust wishes to leave itself within it.

And the Velvet Spiral is always happy to provide an answer.

Another door.

And behind that door, inevitably, is something else to desire.

For Lust is not satisfied by obtaining what it wants.

It is satisfied only long enough to discover what it wants next.
