_As preserved and translated to Common in the [[First Book of Beginnings]], author unknown._

Come closer, little ones, and put another log upon the fire. Tonight, I shall tell you a story older than the mountains, older than the seas, older than the very ground beneath your feet. It is a story my grandmother told me, as her grandmother told her, and as countless grandmothers and grandfathers told their children before them.

It is the story of when there was nothing, and of how, from that nothing, everything came to be.

Listen well, for though this tale speaks of things beyond the understanding of mortal minds, it is still our story. Everything that lives, everything that dies, everything that has ever been and ever shall be owes its existence to the events I shall tell you.

So listen, and remember.

For in the beginning, there was only the Deep.

Not the deep of the oceans, nor the deep of the earth, nor the deep places where sunlight dares not wander. No, children, this was the [[Astral Deep]], an endless expanse without shore or sky, without up or down, without beginning or end. There was no earth upon which to stand, no sun to warm the skin, no stars to guide a traveler home. There was not even darkness, for darkness requires something to hide, and there was nothing to hide.

And yet, the Deep was not empty.

Within its endlessness, something stirred.

We cannot say what first stirred, nor why it did so. Perhaps something had always been there, waiting for a moment that had not yet come. Perhaps the Deep itself had dreamed, and in dreaming, had given birth to the first thoughts of existence. Or perhaps there was no reason at all, and the first things simply came to be.

Whatever the truth, the first of them arose.

They were the original [[Primordials]], the Fundamentals, the living truths of all that could be. Five stood among them as the oldest: [[The Fundamental Five]], whose very nature would come to underpin the existence of all things. They were not born as children are born, nor did they crawl from wombs or hatch from eggs. They simply were, as though the Deep had finally found a way to speak, and its first words had become their flesh.

Among these first beings were **End**, the inevitable conclusion of all things; **Fate**, the unseen hand that bound events to the course of what was yet to come; **Progress**, the ceaseless movement from what is toward what might be; **Hope**, the promise that what is need not be what always shall be; and **Existence**, the simple and profound truth that anything could _be_ at all.

These were the Fundamental Five, though whether they were truly the first, or merely the oldest whose existence could still be remembered, none can say with certainty. Even the wisest scholars cannot agree upon whether any others stood among them, or whether the very notion of first held meaning before the Deep began to change. The Deep keeps its oldest secrets, and perhaps it always shall.

The Fundamentals knew no parents, for none had come before them. They knew no masters, for none stood above them. They had no world to rule, no subjects to command, no purpose to fulfill beyond the simple truth of their odd existence.

End had no last breath to witness.

Fate had no events to bind to a greater course.

Progress had nowhere to go.

Hope had no future to promise.

Existence had nothing beyond itself to encompass.

And so the Fundamentals wandered the endless Deep, if wandering is what one may call movement through a place where no direction existed. They contemplated themselves, and through themselves, they contemplated what might be.

For End understood that a conclusion could not exist without something to conclude. Fate understood that a course could not be followed without events to shape it. Progress understood that there must be a difference between what is and what may yet be. Hope understood that possibility held little meaning without a future in which it might be realized. And Existence understood that, though it alone was, there might yet be something beyond itself.

The Fundamentals discovered that their truths were not solitary things. Each was a part of a greater possibility, and each required the existence of things beyond itself to be fully expressed.

And so, for the first time, the Fundamentals desired something they did not possess.

They desired a world.

No one knows which of them first spoke the desire aloud, nor which first imagined a place where their truths might take shape. Perhaps Progress wished for something new. Perhaps Hope imagined a future that did not yet exist. Perhaps Fate sought a course for events to follow, or End wished for something that could finally reach its conclusion. Perhaps Existence alone understood that there could be more than the endlessness from which they had emerged.

But the desire spread among them, and in time, the Fundamentals turned their attention toward the impossible.

They would make something that was not the Deep.

And from that desire, [[Materia]] was born.

The material world emerged from the endlessness of the Astral Deep, a place where things could possess form, where distances could be measured, where moments could follow one another, and where existence could take on a shape that endured beyond a passing thought.

The first mountains rose. The first waters gathered. The first skies stretched above lands that had never before known a horizon. Light found places to shine, and shadow found places to rest.

For the first time, there was a difference between here and there.

For the first time, there was a place in which something could begin.

And where something could begin, something could end.

The Primordials beheld this new world, and each saw within it the possibility of their own expression. End could now witness the passing of things. Fate could bind events into courses that might be followed. Progress could watch what was become something greater or stranger. Hope could look upon the unrealized future and find meaning in what might yet come to pass. And Existence could behold a reality that was no longer merely itself, but a multitude of things, each possessing its own place within the whole.

Yet Materia was still young, and much of it lay barren.

The Fundamentals looked upon their creation and saw that it lacked the one thing that could make it truly flourish.

Life.

And so some among them took it upon themselves to fill the empty world.

The earliest forests spread their roots into the soil. Strange creatures stirred beneath the waters. Wings beat against the newborn sky, and footsteps marked the first paths through the wilderness. Living things began to hunger, to grow, to struggle, to reproduce, and to die.

Materia was no longer merely a world of stone, water, and sky. It had become a world of happenings, of needs and desires, of beginnings and endings beyond the intentions of its makers.

And the Primordials watched.

At first, the Fundamentals believed themselves to be the only truths that would ever exist. But as life spread, the Deep stirred once more.

New Primordials began to arise.

They were not among the Fundamentals, yet they were no less real. They came into being as life introduced new truths into existence, and the Deep gave those truths form. Hunger arose, and with it came Gluttony, whose appetite would know no easy satisfaction. Growth and decay brought forth forces of renewal and ruin. The struggle between hunter and hunted gave rise to new expressions of predation and survival. As living things formed bonds, built homes, and learned to fear what might be taken from them, still more concepts found their way into the endlessness of the Deep.

Not every new truth was a gentle one.

Some were beautiful. Some were cruel. Some were neither, and simply _were_, as indifferent to the hopes of living creatures as the turning of the seasons would one day become.

The Fundamentals had made a world, and that world had begun to make things of its own.

The Primordials marveled at this, for they had discovered something they had never possessed before: a creation that did not require their hands.

Yet wonder did not remain wonder forever.

The Primordials saw that the creatures of Materia were small, fleeting, and vulnerable. They lived brief lives, made foolish choices, and perished before they could comprehend the truths that governed them. The Fundamentals, who had existed before the first sunrise, could scarcely understand how a creature might fear the setting of the sun.

And so the Primordials took an interest in their creations.

Some offered guidance. Some provided shelter from the terrors of the young world. Some taught the living how to survive, how to build, and how to understand the forces that surrounded them. Others demanded obedience, for they believed that the creatures of Materia owed their existence to those who had made their world possible.

In time, the Primordials became rulers.

The mortals raised monuments to them, offered sacrifices, and spoke their truths in prayers. They begged Progress for prosperity, Hope for deliverance from despair, and Fate for favorable fortune. They pleaded with End to spare those they loved, and with Gluttony to turn its endless hunger toward their enemies rather than themselves. Some prayed to Existence itself, that they might endure against the countless forces that threatened to erase them.

The Fundamentals and those who came after them became the masters of the world, and the mortals became their subjects.

Thus began the Age of Primordial Rule, though the elders who first told this tale knew it by another name: the Age Before the Gods.

For remember, children, that in those days there were no gods as we understand them today. There were only the Primordials, who embodied the truths of existence, and the mortals who lived beneath their shadows.

The Primordials had not yet learned that their creations might one day rise beyond them.

Nor had they learned that a world, once made, might come to belong to those who lived within it.

But that is a tale for another night.

For now, remember this: before there were gods to hear our prayers, there were Primordials whose very existence gave meaning to the words we speak. Before there were heavens to receive the faithful, there was only the endless Deep. And before there were kings, temples, or kingdoms, there was a world that had only just begun to dream.

So long as the Deep endures, the oldest truths shall never truly be forgotten.

And so long as life endures, the Deep may yet dream again.
