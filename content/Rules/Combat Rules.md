#Rules #Homebrew 

# Circumstantial Advantage and Disadvantage
Any time a creature attempts an attack roll, ability check, or saving throw, the DM can assess the current circumstances and decide whether a situational bonus or hindrance applies. The guiding principle should be simple: if the conditions meaningfully help the creature, grant advantage; if they meaningfully hinder it, impose disadvantage. 

The following are examples for how this rule might be applied during combat, but they are not exhaustive:
- **Higher Ground.** A creature that has a clear elevation advantage over its foe—such as fighting from a rampart, a balcony, or atop a boulder—and can exploit that position gains advantage on melee attack rolls against targets below.
- **Precarious Footing.** A creature fighting on a narrow ledge, a slippery ice sheet, or a crumbling beam must contend with its balance. Such a creature has disadvantage on attack rolls and Dexterity-based ability checks while maintaining that position.
- **Environmental Chaos.** In the midst of a storm-tossed sea battle, a driving blizzard, or a magically induced earthquake, all creatures might suffer disadvantage on attack rolls and certain checks, representing the universal difficulty of acting under extreme conditions.
- **Distraction or Deception.** If a foe is momentarily transfixed by a frightening illusion, distracted by a screaming ally, or forced to divide their attention, attackers can gain advantage on their attacks against that distracted target.

Really, this rule exists to make things, such as combat, more dynamic and responsive. Rewarding what could be creative tactics whilst punishing those that might charge heedlessly through difficulty. 

# Starting Combat
#### Balanced Initiative
When combat starts, every participant makes a Dexterity check to determine their place in the initiative order.

However, if the result of the die is equal or lower than their Intelligence modifier, the participant may reroll the d20 once but must take the new result even if it is lower.

#### Ready to Strike
Even if you are outside of combat, you may declare that you are "ready to strike" at any creatures that you can see and observe their behavior for longer than 6 seconds, and you have your weapon readied to Attack at the start of your first combat turn. While Ready to Strike, you can add your proficiency bonus to your Initiative roll.

Any activity other than observing the creatures breaks your concentration and you lose this benefit unless you spend 6 seconds to ready again.

#### Initiative Rush
Sometimes a few points of initiative prevent the cleric from saving the fighter's life before he makes his last death save, or, if a fighter could move up the initiative order, he could act before the enemy and deal the final blow. When you want to achieve such feats, you can rush your initiative.

At any time between your combat turns, you may announce that you want to rush your next turn. If you do so, you can raise your initiative for this round only by any amount up to  
5 + your Dexterity modifier. During your rushed turn you may use only your Movement or your Action but not both, and you cannot use your reaction until the start of your next turn. 

#### Expanded Ready Action
Ready Action states that you can only declare a readied action under the specific condition that "if something happens or someone does something, I will act" where the trigger must happen before you can use your reaction to either move, make an opportunity attack or release a prepared spell. 

You can also make split second statements that say "before something happens, I will do this" where you may attempt to pre-empt the trigger. If you choose such a statement, when your readied action is triggered use your reaction and make an opposed Wisdom (Insight) or Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) skill check (DM chooses the skill) contested by the target's Wisdom (Perception) or Charisma (Deception). You can only make this check using a skill you are proficient in. If you succeed, you can act before the trigger resolves. If you fail, your used reaction is forfeited but your Readied Action can be used after the current turn ends.

# Generics of Combat
### A Turn in Combat
- **Movement.** A creature can move a number of feet per round equal to its speed once per turn.
- **Full Action.** A Full Action requires 1 Action, 1 Bonus Action, 1 Reaction, and all Movement.
- **Long Action.** A Long Action costs 1 Action and 1 Bonus Action.
- **Action.** 1 per turn. Actions can also be used to perform any bonus action or free action.
- **Bonus Action.** 1 per turn. Bonus actions can also be used to perform any free action.
- **Reaction.** 1 per round, can't be taken on the creature's own turn.
- **Free Action.** 6 per round. can be taken at any time.

### Free Item Interaction
At any time during your combat turn, you have one free interaction with your inventory or the environment. You can draw a one-handed or two-handed weapon, or draw any Prepared items from worn containers. You can also interact with one feature of the environment (such as opening or closing a door, kicking a table).

If you want to interact with a second object, draw an item from a backpack container, manipulate an object such as magic item or a complex activity that requires an Ability check, you need to use your action.

### Grappling
When using the Grapple action, the attacker's attack roll is no longer contested by the target's Strength (Athletics) check or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check, but is made against a DC equal to 8 + Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) skill modifier of the target (its choice).

If the target has an advantage to resist a grapple the DC is increased by 3, or decreased by 3 if it suffers a disadvantage instead.

### Escaping a Grapple
Instead of your whole action, you may use any of your attacks to attempt an escape from grapple. To attempt an Escape, make a Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check against the Grapple DC (8 + Strength (Athletics) modifier) of the creature grappling you. If you succeed, you lose the Grappled condition. If your roll a natural 1 or your result is an exceptional failure, you fail and can't attempt any more Escape attempts until your next turn.

Grappled creatures with the Multiattack ability may use this ability as Multiescape instead. For each attack described as part of the Multiattack, they get one escape attempt. If the Multiattack includes more than one set of attacks, use the set with the most attacks.

### Disarming
When using the Disarm action, the attacker's attack roll is no longer contested by the target's Strength (Athletics) check or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check, but is made against a DC equal to 10 + Strength Saving Throw or Dexterity Saving Throw modifier of the target (its choice).

The attacker has advantage on their attack roll if they grappled using **Opportunity Grapple** or with a weapon with the **Grappling** property or if they are larger than the target. The attacker has disadvantage on their attack roll if the target is holding the item with two or more hands, or they suffer a level of disadvantage for if they are smaller than the target for every level smaller.

If you succeed, the weapon, shield or item is no longer held by the target. You can use your item interaction to either kick the weapon into an adjacent area (your choice) or take the item if you have a free hand, otherwise the target may use its item interaction on their turn to take it back.

### Delay
On your turn, you can choose to take the Delay action. If you do so, you cannot move, take an action, or use a bonus action on that turn. You can still use your reaction normally while you are delaying, unless you later use your delayed turn. You may end your delay at any point in the same round. To do so, you must declare your intention to act immediately after another creature's turn resolves and before the next turn begins. When you do, you take your full turn (including movement, actions, and any bonus actions) in that gap. 

Acting in this manner permanently changes your place in the initiative order. Your new initiative count becomes equal to the count of the creature you immediately acted after, and for the remainder of the combat, you take your turns directly following that creature. If the round ends before you have chosen to act, you lose the delayed turn entirely.

### Squeezing
A creature can share a 5-foot square (or larger space) with one or more willing allies, but doing so imposes drawbacks. You cannot voluntarily end your movement in a space occupied by a hostile creature; if you are forced into such a space, the normal squeezing rules apply.

The number of creatures that can effectively share a space and still fight or cast spells depends on their size:
- **Medium Creatures.** Up to two Medium creatures can share a 5-foot square while suffering penalties.
- **Small Creatures.** Up to three Small creatures can share a 5-foot square while suffering penalties.
- **Tiny Creatures.** Up to four Tiny creatures can occupy a 5-foot square without penalty. Up to eight Tiny creatures can share that same space with the standard penalties. Any more than that , and the group is best treated as a swarm rather than as individual combatants.
The following restrictions apply until the creatures are no longer sharing that space:
- Disadvantage on attack rolls.
- Disadvantage on Dexterity saving throws.
- Disadvantage on ability checks that use Dexterity.
- Attack rolls made against it have advantage.
- If the allies in the space are of different sizes, the larger creature provides half cover to the smaller one.
Certain traits, such as halfling's Nimbleness trait, allow a creature to ignore the penalties listed above while still benefiting from the half cover.

### Everyone is an Ambusher
When you make an attack roll against a creature that is currently surprised, you have advantage on that attack roll. This benefit applies to every attack you make against that creature until its first turn in the initiative order ends, at which point it is no longer surprised and can defend itself normally.

Because this rule grants advantage on ambush attacks, rogues can reliably apply their Sneak Attack damage during the opening volley of combat. This rule also augments the Ambusher trait and similar abilities based on the DM's discretion. For example, a monster may be granted an additional bonus action on its first turn against surprised creatures.

### Flanking
A creature is flanked when it is threatened by two or more allied creatures that are positioned on exact opposite sides or corners of its space. To gain this benefit, all flanking creatures must be capable of making melee attacks and must not be incapacitated.

When you and an ally are flanking a creature, you both gain advantage on melee attack rolls against that creature, This benefit applies to each attack you make while maintaining the flanking position. The target, caught between two threats, cannot fully focus its defenses on either assailant. Flanking can only be achieved with melee attacks by default. Ranged weapon attacks do not benefit from flanking.

### Allies, Cover, and Ranged Combat
When you make a ranged attack against a creature that is within 5 feet of at least one of your allies and that ally is not incapacitated, you have disadvantage on the attack roll. The reason for this is that you must take care to avoid striking your comrade as the combatants shift and struggle. However, if the target is positioned directly between you and your ally, you can make the attack as normal.

If your ally is interposed between you and your target, you still attack with disadvantage. Additionally, if your attack roll misses the target but the total roll is high enough to hit your ally's Armor Class, the attack strikes your ally instead. Additionally, the target is counted as having cover, the cover being your ally. 

# Progressive Death Saves
When you are reduced to 0 hit points, you begin making death saving throws as normal. However, the number of failed death saves you have accumulated determines your current state, as outlined below:

| **Failed Saves**      | **Condition**        | **Effect**                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0                     | Prone (Cannot Stand) | You have collapsed from exhaustion. You cannot stand up, but your speed is not reduced to 0 as you can crawl and take actions, including attacks at disadvantage, in a desperate bid to defend yourself.   |
| 1                     | Incapacitated        | You have lost the strength to fight meaningfully. You cannot take actions or reactions, but you can still crawl, speak, and interact with objects (such as grabbing a McGuffin or delivering final words). |
| More Fails than Saves | Unconscious          | You are unresponsive and unaware of your surroundings. You cannot move, act, or speak. This condition visibly signals to allies that you are in dire straits.                                              |
| 3                     | Dead                 | You have succumbed to your injuries.                                                                                                                                                                       |

At the moment you drop to 0 hit points, you may choose to voluntarily end concentration on any spell you are maintaining. This is not required but is often prudent, as maintaining concentration while prone, incapacitated, or unconscious is nearly impossible under these conditions.

When you accumulate three successful death saves, you stabilize. However, you do not automatically regain consciousness or shed the conditions imposed by your failed saves. You remain in whatever state your current failed save total dictates and you must be healed or tended to before you can stand, act, or speak normally. A successful DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine) check or any healing (even 1 hit point) removes all death save conditions and returns you to full functionality. However, regaining hit points after having been downed causes you to suffer the [[Conditions & Effects#Staggered|Staggered]] condition.
