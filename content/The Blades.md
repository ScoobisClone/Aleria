#Lore 
# The Five Blades
## Exact Blade
The Exact Blade is the foundation upon which all civilized swordplay is built. Practitioners of this school approach the blade as both an art and a discipline, favoring clean lines, balanced stances, and an unwavering sense of control. Each movement is deliberate, each strike measured, nothing ever wasted. The blade is always in alignment with the body, the spine straight, the gaze unshaken. These swordsmen often speak of something they call "the true line," the perfect distance at which one may strike, defend, or withdraw with equal efficiency in all. This is a style forged for dueling courts and military academies, where efficiency is highly valued. Those who wield this style do not flinch or gamble, relying solely on their logic.

## Swift Blade
Speed is the soul of the Swift Blade, and its swordsmen live in motion like hawks in flight. There is no standing still, nor trading blows, only the rush of wind and the clash of steel. The practitioners of this style train not only in technique, but in reflex and distance. Their footwork is tireless, their blades light, their attacks in rapid bursts. The ideal duel ends before the enemy realizes it has begun, a blur of movement. Dodging is much favored over blocking, as is repositioning over resistance. Swift Blades focus on dancing in and out of reach, scoring wounds while denying retaliation. They thrive in open terrain, narrow alleys, and large battlefields. Their body is the storm cloud, their blade the lightning.

## Crushing Blade
The Crushing Blade is not for the faint of arm, nor the weak of heart. It is a path of crushing blows and brute will, casting aside the elegance of finesse in favor of raw power. Its warriors are iron-boned and grim-eyed, standing with legs wide and center low, grounding themselves like pillars before unleashing devastating cleaving arcs of destruction. Every motion carries weight, every strike is a sentence. There is no feint, no pretense: only the full commitment of steel and muscle. Now, timing matters, yes, but more in the rhythm of demolition than something like the dance of fencing. Guards are broken, weapons sundered, and opponents driven to the ground under the sheer pressure of force. Crushing Blade practitioners do not fight to impress, as their strikes are each meant to end things. Their sword a boulder, their body a cliff.

## Gentle Blade
The Gentle Blade is neither weak nor passive, though many mistake it as such. It is the path of control, of using the enemy's force against them, and of choosing not to kill when a victory can be achieved without bloodshed. Those who practice this style yield, absorb, and redirect. A strike met with the Gentle Blade is not blocked but instead turned aside, often in attempts to redirect the blade back to its owner. The blade may not be quick, nor heavy, but it is always where it needs to be. The Gentle Blade teaches a sort of patience, awareness, and humility as it prizes the disarm over the strike, the bind over the break. Many guardians and temple fighters practice this style, placing more trust in their discipline than their aggression. Their sword is the eye, their swings the storm.

## Bewitching Blade
The Bewitched Blade is not named for actual sorcery, but for the illusions of the mind, the sleight of hand, and rhythm that can turn a blade into a question rather than an answer. It is the art of deception made steel. Those who practice this style avoid meeting opponents directly; rather, they choose to invite, mislead, and exploit. Stances shift irregularly, grips suddenly reverse, and movements appear irregular. One may never know if a strike is true or simply a feint, just as if an opening is genuine or a trap. It is a school born in alleys, prisons, and those with theatrical natures, wherever being unpredictable could lead to your survival. It is most feared in duels rather than group fights, as a single wrong assumption could result in death. A Bewitched Blade never reveals intent. Instead, they mirror the thoughts of the foe and disrupt them. Their sword is a whisper, their actions a riddle.

# Unwritten Blades
## Binding Blade
The Binding Blade is a path that focuses on catching, rather than cutting. Where other swordsmen seek the perfect distance, the Binding Blade denies distance altogether. Practitioners surge past the lethal edge, nigh pressing their bodies against their opponent's guard, converting their sword into a lever, a hook, a cage of interlocking steel. It is this blade's belief that a fight is not necessarily won via a slice across the throat, as a pommel driven into the temple can achieve victory as well. Every motion is a prelude to a grapple. These warriors speak of "the clinch," that suffocating distance where reach is irrelevant and a weapon often becomes no more than a tool for joint-breaking instead of swinging. This is a school born from tunnels and tight city streets, where there is no room to dance and no honor in keeping one's distance. Their sword is a lock, their body a cage.

## Unending Blade
The Unending Blade is not a single strike, nor even a series of them, for it is a current. Where the Swift Blade is akin to strikes of lightning, the Unending Blade is better described as a tidal wave. Practitioners of this school reject the finality of the one-cut kill entirely, favoring instead a relentless cascade of steel that gives the enemy no breath, no respite, not even a moment to think. A cut becomes a hook, the hook becomes a thrust, the thrust spirals into a rising slash, and the slash crashes into an overhand (each movement purposely built from the rebound of the last). There is no reset, no pause to reassess; only the increasing weight of the unbroken pressure. As such, their footwork is a steady advance, their blades never still, their attacks a tide that erodes guard and will alike. While often confused with the Swift Blade by laymen, these blades do not thrive in sudden bursts, but in the slow drowning of an opponent who realizes too late that every parry of theirs only feeds the next blow. This style is favored among elves and those with great patience. Their steel is a current, their rhythm a tide that waits for no shore.

## Feral Blade
The Feral Blade is not a philosophy of honor, nor a real doctrine of form. Its practitioners have learned through scar and scream that the duel is a lie told by those who have never been truly desperate. They use dirt in the eyes, a kick to the kneecap, a sacrificial forearm to catch a blade, or even a corpse as a weapon. Feigning death is a tactic; biting is a tactic. Screaming to break concentration is a tactic. There is no guard, no stance, no rhythm that cannot be abandoned all for the sake of a victory, even if an ugly one. Feral Blades train in chaos and improvisation, as these techniques usually make use of reading terrain and the sort of leverage found when a predator reads prey. It is a blade forged in prisons, gladiator pits, and war, wherever the luxury of a fair fight can be a fatal delusion. Their sword is a claw, their fight a frenzy that knows no whistle.

## Bastion Blade
The Bastion Blade is an art of drawing lines. Where the Five Blades concern themselves with personal victory, the Bastion Blade concerns itself with ground, with allies, and with the space that must be held at all costs. Its practitioners stand wide and low, their off-hand armed with buckler, cloak, shield, or some other defensive option, their cuts sweeping in broad arcs that deny approach to multiple enemies at once. These techniques seek territory, carving the air to keep that territory inviolate. Their footwork is measured and deliberate, advancing only in lockstep with formation, retreating only when their line demands it. This style came about among the dwarves and spread amongst other races as a means of defensive combat. As such, it is popular amongst those like city watchmen. Bastion practitioners typically fight so that those behind them need not. Their steel is a wall, their stance a fortress that does not crumble.

# Origins
Long ago, during the [[Rise of Man]], there walked a singular figure known only as the Blade King. With him, he founded what are known as five perfect techniques that would go on to affect mortal warfare to the present day. These were the original **Five Blades**, and in this time, the King declared them the only honest expressions of steel. His teachings swept across the fledgling kingdoms of Man, and for generations, his words remained iron law. To stray from the Five was to embrace barbarism.

Yet, the Blade King was mortal, and upon his vanishing, the techniques themselves were lost to time. The true forms became dust and legend.

What endured, however, were his principles. Bereft of the original model, generations of swordsmen, scholars, and duelists rebuilt the art from fragmentary texts, old drills, and oral tradition. Over centuries, these constant reconstructions crystallized into the modern five great philosophical branches of steel: the **Exact Blade** of precision and measure, the **Swift Blade** of speed and reaction, the **Crushing Blade** of strength and commitment, the **Gentle Blade** of flow and redirection, and the **Bewitching Blade** of deception and misdirection.

Today, these Five are recognized by modern warriors as the five true paths to combat, the root from which most martial traditions grow. Every stance, every lunge, every desperate swing can be traced to one or more of these root essences. Now, the Five Blades are not rigid fighting styles in the modern sense. A more apt description would be to consider them five colors of the martial spectrum. A warrior typically no longer adheres to a single branch, as their technique may use the lunge of the Swift Blade as well as the overhead of the Crushing Blade. A modern style is merely a ratio of these ingredients, mixed to fit the specific needs that caused the style to be created.

But the world did not begin, nor will it end, with Man.

As humanity's empires spread, they encountered races whose bones, battlefields, and blood did not necessarily bow to the King's philosophy. The Blade King, after all, forged his teachings for the purpose of harming fellow man. His forms assumed two arms, two legs, human reach, human posture, and the rhythm of a human heartbeat. The dwarves, constantly tunneling through their stone-choked warrens, found the Five too rigid and too long for their ways. The elves, whose lives expanded across centuries, saw the Five as impatient, far too obsessed with the finality of a mere single exchange when a battle might unfold over the course of an hourglass. The greenskins, born of ceaseless struggle and pragmatic bloodshed, dismissed the Five as the luxury of scholars who had never learned combat earnestly. And the beast-folk, alongside their fellow races, viewed the Blade King's "civilized" paths as nothing more than a narrow cage built for an equally narrow species.

Thus, while the human academies stubbornly clung to the orthodox Five, the rest of the world took the King's teachings and made them their own, filtering what can be considered their essences through different flesh, environments, and philosophies. The human academies, ever proud and ever rigid, refuse to acknowledge these adaptations as legitimate. They call them the "**Unwritten Blades**." Other terms are thrown around as well, such as "Beast-Tricks" or the "Mud-Paths." To name them formally, in the orthodox view, is to dignify blasphemy. The purists claim these divergent forms are crude adaptations born of inferior biology, improper temperaments, or some fundamental issue, considering the Unwritten Blades as nothing more than dirt added to a pristine lake of water.
