# The Five Firstborn
## Existence - Ozaeryth,  the Absolute
*The truth that anything is at all.*

Existence is the most fundamental of the Firstborn. It embodies the fact that anything can be, from the endless [[Astral Deep]] to the smallest grain of sand within [[Materia]]. It is neither creation nor life, but the truth that allows either to exist.

Before there could be a world, a beginning, or an ending, there was Existence. It is the foundation upon which all other truths rest, though whether Existence itself was the first to arise or merely the first that can be understood is unknown.

## End - Yndrimel, the End
*The truth that all things may cease.*

End embodies conclusion, cessation, and finality. It is not merely death, destruction, or oblivion, but the principle that anything which exists may eventually reach a point beyond which it continues no further.

A journey may end without its traveler dying. An era may end without the world being destroyed. Even a possibility may end before it is realized. End is the truth that makes them possible.

Without End, nothing could ever be finished, and no conclusion could ever be final.

## Fate - Azhryvael, the Unturning
*The truth that existence unfolds.*

Fate embodies the course of events: the passage from one moment to the next, the relationship between what has occurred and what follows, and the unfolding of existence through time. It need not dictate every outcome or prescribe an unalterable future. Rather, it represents the principle that existence can have a course at all.

Through Fate, events may follow one another, consequences may arise from causes, and the present may give way to a future. Whether every course is predetermined, or whether Fate merely encompasses the paths that events may take, remains a matter of dispute.

Without Fate, there would be no unfolding of events, no course for existence to follow, and no meaningful distinction between what has been and what is yet to come.

## Progress - Xyrathovel, the Beyond
*The truth that what is may become more.*

Progress embodies movement from one state toward another, the emergence of what was not previously present, and the possibility of reaching states beyond the current one. Progress concerns the movement along that course toward something different.

Progress does not necessarily mean improvement. A civilization may advance toward ruin, a creature may grow into something monstrous, and a discovery may lead to consequences worse than ignorance. Progress is neither benevolent nor malicious; it is the principle that existence may advance beyond its present state.

Without Progress, there could be no development, no advancement, and no emergence of something greater, stranger, or simply different from what came before.

## Hope - Nymerielle, the Dawn
*The truth that what may be can be desired.*

Hope embodies the significance of an unrealized future: the possibility that what exists need not be the final state of things, and that another outcome may be worth reaching for. It is not merely possibility itself, nor is it limited to the emotion felt by living creatures. At its most fundamental, Hope is the principle that a future beyond the present can hold meaning.

Where Progress concerns movement toward another state, Hope concerns the promise that such a state might be worth attaining. It does not guarantee success, nor does it ensure that the desired future will ever come to pass. Hope can persist in the face of impossible odds, and it can be misplaced, betrayed, or extinguished.

Without Hope, there might still be change and advancement, but nothing would embody the significance of a future yet unrealized.
