#Rules
# Universal Weapon Properties
###### **Adamantine.**
Weapons forged from the legendary dark metal are nigh‑indestructible, their edges capable of sundering stone and steel alike. Such a weapon deals double damage to objects, and when applied to a melee weapon with the versatile or heavy property, its damage die increases by two steps as its weight and density become a terrible advantage.

###### **AP (Armor Penetration).**
Designed to defeat even the most resilient defenses, these weapons ignore a target’s resistance to their damage type; immunity becomes mere resistance, and if the target has no resistance at all, it becomes vulnerable to the attack. Greater variants (AP+, AP++) ignore resistance and immunity entirely, and the rarest form bypasses all damage reduction conferred by armor itself. These effects do not stack with multiple sources.

###### **Assist.**
A weapon with the Assist property is one that was designed to aid in defending the wielder, yet its usage makes it cumbersome to wield anything too big at the same time. While wielding an Assist weapon you gain a +1 bonus modifier to your Armor Class, but you cannot benefit from wielding Heavy weapons or a shield.

###### **Blessed.**
Infused with holy power, this weapon glows with a soft radiance that sears the corrupt and the damned. On a hit, it deals an extra 1d6 radiant damage—or 1d12 against fiends and undead, who recoil from its purifying touch.

###### **Collapsible.**
Engineered for the discreet warrior, this weapon can be folded, telescoped, or otherwise reduced in size for easy concealment. While stowed, you have advantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks made to hide it about your person.

###### **Cursed.**
Bearing the taint of shadow and spite, this weapon drinks the life force of the living. On a hit, it deals an extra 1d6 necrotic damage—or 1d12 against celestials and fey, whose bright natures are particularly vulnerable to its malice.

###### **Dark Kiss.**
A weapon of vile enchantment that drains not merely blood but the very essence of vitality. The target’s maximum hit points are reduced by the damage dealt; this withering persists until the target completes a long rest and regains its lost vigor.

###### **Exotic.**
Weapons of unusual make and demanding technique—foreign blades, intricate chain‑whips, or peculiar firearms—are considered exotic. They are difficult to master but often deal increased damage compared to their martial counterparts, rewarding the dedicated student. These weapons require special proficiencies.

###### **Finesse.**
Balanced for agility rather than brute force, a finesse weapon rewards speed and precision. You may use your Dexterity modifier instead of Strength for attack and damage rolls with this weapon.

###### **Glowing ().**
Some weapons are forged with luminous materials or enchanted to shed their own light, banishing shadows and revealing hidden foes. This weapon sheds bright light in a radius given in parentheses, and dim light for an equal additional distance.

###### **Heavy.**
Massive and unwieldy, heavy weapons require great strength to control; Small creatures have disadvantage on attack rolls with them. Moreover, if you move and then fire a heavy ranged weapon, you do so at disadvantage, and if you fire before moving, you forfeit your movement that turn.

###### **High Crit.**
Some weapons are designed to exploit a single, devastating opening with ruthless efficiency. When you score a critical hit with this weapon, triple the damage dice rather than doubling them.

###### **Launcher ().**
A weapon with the Launcher property is fitted with a mechanism that can fire or propel another weapon, making each shot a double threat. It launches another weapon (the number in parentheses indicates how many or the type), dealing both the launching weapon's damage and the launched weapon's damage on a hit.

###### **Life‑Steal.**
These hungry weapons drain the vigor of their victims and transfer a measure of that stolen life to the wielder. You gain temporary hit points equal to the necrotic damage dealt by this weapon.

###### **Light.**
Small, nimble, and easily maneuvered, a light weapon is ideal for two‑weapon fighting, allowing an off‑hand strike without penalty.

###### **Mage Hunter.**
Forged in manners with hatred to the arcane, this weapon was crafted to disrupt spellcasters. You have advantage on attack rolls against any creature within range that is concentrating on a spell.

###### **Magnetic.**
Reinforced with lodestone and bound with electric charge, this weapon is drawn to metal as surely as a compass to north. It deals an extra 1d4 lightning damage and grants a +1d4 bonus to attack rolls against creatures wearing metal armor or composed primarily of metal.

###### **Noxious.**
Coated in venomous reservoirs or forged from toxic materials, this weapon delivers a sickening wound. On a hit, it deals an extra 1d8 poison damage, leaving its victims weakened and retching.

###### **Primordial ().**
Channeling the raw chaos of the elemental planes, this weapon crackles with energy. On a hit, it deals an extra 1d6 damage of a chosen type (fire, lightning, cold, acid, or thunder), chosen when the weapon is created or attuned.

###### **Range (/).**
A weapon that can be used to make a ranged attack has a range in parentheses after the ammunition or thrown property. The range lists two numbers. The first is the weapon’s normal range in feet, and the second indicates the weapon’s long range. When attacking a target beyond normal range, you have disadvantage on the attack roll. You can’t attack a target beyond the weapon’s long range.

###### **Resonant.**
Forged to hum with psychic frequencies, this weapon assails the mind as much as the body. On a hit, it deals an extra 1d4 psychic damage; on a critical hit, this increases to 1d10 as the mental impact reverberates.

###### **Seeking.**
Enchanted to correct its own trajectory, this weapon rarely misses entirely. Once per turn, when you make an attack without disadvantage and miss, you instead hit and deal minimum damage.

###### **Siege Weapon.**
Built to tear down fortifications, this weapon is the bane of walls and war‑machines. It deals double damage to objects, structures, and constructs.

###### **Stunning ().**
Designed to concuss and disorient, a stunning weapon leaves its target reeling. A creature hit must make a Constitution save (DC = 8 + the number in parentheses) or be Stunned until the start of your next turn.

###### **Switch ().**
A marvel of mechanical engineering, this weapon has two distinct forms; the damage and properties of the second are listed in parentheses. You may swap between forms as if drawing a weapon, adapting to the changing tide of battle.

###### **Thrown (/).**
Intended to be cast at the enemy, this weapon may be thrown as a ranged attack; the range is given in parentheses, allowing the wielder to strike from afar.

###### **Two‑Handed.**
Requiring the full strength of both arms, a two‑handed weapon is devastating but cumbersome; it can be used with one hand only at disadvantage, making it a poor choice for the injured or the careless.

###### **Volatile.**
Unstable and dangerous, this weapon is a bomb waiting to be triggered. If it is destroyed, it immediately triggers its effect—often a destructive explosion, to the misfortune of all nearby.

# Melee‑Specific Weapon Properties
###### **Balanced.**
Despite its imposing size, this weapon has been counter‑weighted and refined for nimble use. It can be wielded by Small creatures without the usual disadvantage imposed by the heavy property, for it moves with surprising grace.

###### **Brutal.**
A weapon of unsubtle violence, its every blow crushes and breaks. On a critical hit, this weapon rolls two additional damage dice, savaging the target beyond measure.

###### **Cavalry.**
Designed for mounted combat, this weapon is easily handled from the saddle; it requires one hand while mounted, but two hands when fighting on foot, where its length and weight become unwieldy.

###### **Cleaving.**
Forged to split flesh and bone, this blade continues its arc through a victim's body with terrible momentum. On a critical hit, the target takes an extra 4d6 slashing damage; if you roll a natural 20 and then another 20, you sever a limb (or equivalent body part), leaving a lasting mark.

###### **Counterweighted.**
Ordinarily a two‑handed weapon, this armament has been weighted with leaden pommels and balanced rings to allow single‑handed use by the exceptionally strong. If your Strength score is 17 or higher, you can wield it in one hand.

###### **Crushing.**
Each bone‑shattering blow from this weapon deforms armor and leaves its wearer exposed. Each hit reduces the target’s Armor Class by 1 (minimum 10) until they complete a short or long rest and hammer out the dents.

###### **Deadblow.**
A blunt instrument of terrible force, the deadblow weapon caves in armor and sends enemies staggering. It deals an extra 1d4 damage and automatically shoves any Large or smaller creature up to 10 feet away from you, clearing a path through the press of battle.

###### **Double.**
This weapon bears two damage‑dealing ends, each as deadly as the other. When you take the Attack action, you may use your bonus action to make an additional attack with the other end, but you do not add your ability modifier to that extra attack’s damage, for the second strike is an afterthought to the first.

###### **Draw.**
A weapon with the Draw property is one meant to deliver its full force when unsheathed instead of over various strikes. While sheathed, the next attack made with a Draw weapon gains a bonus modifier to hit equal to half its wielder's proficiency bonus, rounded down, and deals an additional damage die on a hit. After being unsheathed, its wielder may spend one of their attacks as part of the Attack action to sheathe it.

###### **Earthshatter.**
A weapon that strikes with the force of a falling boulder, its impacts send foes tumbling. On a hit, the target must make a Strength saving throw or be knocked prone, their footing utterly destroyed.

###### **Elegant.**
A blade of exquisite balance and delicate weight, this weapon requires more than mere strength. You must have a Dexterity score of 16 or higher to wield it, for its precise arcs reward only the nimble.

###### **Fist.**
Designed to be worn or wielded as an extension of the body, this weapon makes attacks that are treated as unarmed strikes; its damage die decreases by one step, but it allows a warrior to integrate weapons into their natural fighting style.

###### **Grapple.**
Adorned with hooks, barbs, or jagged edges, this weapon can be driven into an opponent and used to control them. You can add your weapon’s damage roll to any grapple check you make, twisting the blade to lend weight to your hold.

###### **Keen.**
Honed to a razor edge, this weapon finds the weakest points in armor with uncanny frequency. It scores a critical hit on a roll of 19 or 20, its edge seeking out vital gaps.

###### **Knockback ().**
The sheer force of a Knockback weapon sends foes stumbling, their footing lost against the violence of the blow. A creature hit must make a Strength save (DC = 8 + proficiency bonus, if proficient, + the number in parentheses) or be knocked prone and pushed back 5 feet per point in that number. If this would force them off a ledge, they may make a Dexterity save to arrest their momentum at the edge.

###### **Lacerating.**
Its edges are so sharp that even a glancing cut opens a grievous wound. When you deal maximum slashing damage with this weapon, the target gains one level of exhaustion, their body struggling to cope with the trauma.

###### **Large.**
So big that its mere heft hampers its aim, this weapon is a menace to friend and foe alike in tight quarters. Attacks with this weapon suffer a –3 penalty to attack rolls in tight spaces.

###### **Massive.**
This is a weapon of monstrous proportions, requiring immense strength to even lift, let alone swing. It increases its damage by two steps, but you can only attack once per turn with it—if you would have multiple attacks, each extra attack adds two extra damage dice to the single, ponderous blow.

###### **Melee Caster.**
Infused with arcane resonance, this weapon responds to the wielder's magical nature as readily as to their physical strength. You may use your Spellcasting Ability Modifier for attack and damage rolls, treating the blade as a focus for your power.

###### **Mithral (Melee).**
Forged from the legendary silver‑grey metal, this finesse weapon is impossibly light and strong. Its damage die increases by two steps and it weighs half as much as a steel equivalent, allowing for blindingly fast strikes.

###### **Paired.**
This weapon is never found alone; it comes with a twin, crafted to be wielded together. You can draw or stow both simultaneously, and they are ideal for two‑weapon fighting, Although if one is lost, the remaining weapon loses this property.

###### **Parry.**
Designed with a guard that can deflect incoming attacks, this weapon serves as both sword and buckler. As a reaction, you may gain +1 Armor Class against one incoming attack that you can see, turning the blade aside.

###### **Power.**
A weapon that hums with latent energy, its critical strikes are monstrously amplified. On a critical hit, this weapon rolls one additional damage die as the energy discharges in a burst of force.

###### **Reach.**
Weapons with the Reach property are built long, their hafts or chains extending the wielder's threat far beyond the norm. Your melee reach with this weapon is extended by 5 feet.

###### **Returning.**
Enchanted to return to its owner, this thrown weapon never stays in the enemy's grasp for long. After being thrown, it flies back to your hand at the end of your turn, ready to be cast again.

###### **Serrated.**
Its jagged teeth tear flesh and grind against bone, leaving wounds that bleed freely. If a creature takes damage from a serrated weapon twice in a single turn, it suffers an additional 1d12 slashing damage as the sawing motion opens a terrible gash.

###### **Superheavy.**
Of such enormous size that it dwarfs even the largest greatswords, this weapon is a siege engine in the hands of a mortal. You must have a Strength score of 16 or higher to wield it proficiently, for its weight demands extraordinary muscle.

###### **Swift.**
A blade of lightning reflexes, this weapon moves faster than the eye can follow. After the Attack action, you may make a bonus action attack; if you wield two swift weapons, you can make two bonus action attacks, a blur of steel.

###### **Threatening.**
So long and aggressive is this weapon that enemies cannot safely turn their backs on it. It can make opportunity attacks without consuming your reaction, for its reach and menace are ever‑present.

###### **Trip.**
Designed to hook and unbalance, this weapon is a favorite of gladiators and scouts. When you hit, instead of dealing damage, you may immediately use a bonus action to attempt to shove the target prone, with advantage on the shove attempt, sending them sprawling.

###### **Versatile.**
A weapon that can be wielded in one hand or two, its strength grows with the wielder's grip. It may be used one‑handed or two‑handed; when two‑handed, its damage die increases by one step, the extra leverage adding force to every blow.

# Ranged‑Specific Weapon Properties
###### **Ammunition.**
A weapon that depends on a supply of physical projectiles—arrows, bolts, bullets, or more exotic missiles—cannot be used without them.

###### **Assault.**
A weapon built for aggressive, mobile warfare, it can be fired even after a headlong rush. If you dash this turn, you may still make an Attack action with this weapon, but suffer a –3 penalty to all attack rolls that turn as you struggle to regain your aim.

###### **Automatic (/).**
These weapons are capable of terrifying rates of fire, offering three distinct modes: _spray_ (saturating an area), _focus fire_ (concentrating on a single target), and _controlled bursts_ (measured, normal attacks). Each mode consumes ammunition equal to the number in parentheses multiplied by itself.

###### **Blast().**
When activated, this weapon unleashes a sphere of destruction whose radius is given in parentheses. All creatures within that space must make a Dexterity saving throw or take full damage, for the blast cares little for friend or foe.

###### **Burst Fire ().**
A middle ground between control and volume, this mode allows one normal attack per attack you have, consuming a number of rounds equal to the number in parentheses per attack, putting a steady stream of lead into the air.

###### **Combi.**
A crafty invention, this weapon houses a secondary firing mode—often a different caliber or payload. During your Attack action, you may use that mode for one attack, or gain one extra attack with it at the cost of –5 to all attacks that turn, as the mechanism's complexity throws off your rhythm.

###### **Deviation ().**
Inaccurate but powerful, this weapon is aimed at a point rather than a person. Nominate a point within range; make an attack roll against DC 10. On a miss, roll the scatter die (1d6) and a die of the size given in parentheses (minus your Dexterity bonus, minimum 1), and the shot deviates that many 5‑foot increments in the indicated direction.

###### **Engulf.**
This weapon projects a cone of devastation of the given size, blanketing the area in flames, acid, or other horrors. All creatures in the cone must make a Dexterity saving throw or take full damage, their only hope to dive clear.

###### **Extensive Action.**
A weapon so complex or so slow that it requires the wielder's full attention for each shot. Regardless of extra attacks, you may only attack once per Attack action with this weapon.

###### **Foregrip.**
Fitted with an additional handgrip, this weapon may be braced or wielded with one or two hands; when two‑handed, its normal and long ranges double, the stability improving your reach.

###### **Ignite.**
These weapons leave lingering flames. If you deal damage to a creature wearing flammable material, it catches fire, gaining 1 stack of Burn.

###### **Indirect.**
Designed to fire over obstacles, this weapon can strike targets out of line of sight, lobbing projectiles in high arcs. However, it counts as having the [[#**Deviation ().**|Deviation]] property with the deviation dice doubled and targets gain advantage on saves against it; it may not be used indoors, where the ceiling robs it of its trajectory.

###### **Loading.**
A weapon that must be primed before each shot, its loading mechanism is time‑consuming. Reloading takes a bonus action, though some variants require an action to fully reload.

###### **Long Gun.**
A weapon of considerable length and weight, it is difficult to steady without support. It is fired at disadvantage unless you use your bonus action to brace it against a solid surface or go prone, sacrificing mobility for accuracy.

###### **Massive Parts.**
A weapon of truly enormous scale, its mechanisms are the size of a man's torso. It requires two actions to reload and two actions to set up or take down, and is best left to artillery crews.

###### **Misfire ().**
Inherently unreliable, this weapon is as likely to fail as to fire. If the attack roll is equal to or less than the misfire score, the attack misses and you roll on the misfire chart—from a simple jam to catastrophic destruction. A reliable weapon allows rolling twice and taking the higher result.

###### **Mounted.**
Normally used on a tripod or vehicle, this weapon is too heavy for infantry. Medium creatures have disadvantage when unmounted; Small or smaller creatures cannot wield it at all, its bulk overwhelming them.

###### **Overheat.**
A weapon that pushes its mechanisms to the breaking point, delivering devastating power at the cost of cooldown. It deals two extra steps of damage, but after each attack it cannot be used again until the end of your next turn, its barrel glowing white‑hot.

###### **Penetrating.**
The projectile is designed to punch through multiple targets, retaining its lethal energy. You may attack all creatures in a straight line within normal range; each must succeed on a Dexterity saving throw or take the weapon’s damage, the shot drilling through rank after rank.

###### **Pistol.**
A small, handy weapon meant for close-quarters work. It does not suffer disadvantage when used against a target within 5 feet, its compact size making it easy to turn and fire in a confined space.

###### **Powder.**
This weapon relies on traditional gunpowder and requires a powder horn to load, functioning like a spell component pouch.

###### **Rapid‑fire.**
A weapon that rewards aggression, its rate of fire increasing at close quarters. If fired within half its short range, this weapon allows one extra attack when you use the Attack action, the enemy too close to evade the barrage.

###### **Reliable.**
Crafted with exceptional care, this weapon rarely suffers from the malfunctions that plague lesser arms. When forced to roll on the misfire chart, roll twice and take the higher result.

###### **Reload (/).**
These weapons must be fed ammunition between volleys, their mechanisms clicking and clanking as fresh rounds are chambered. A Reload weapon can be fired a limited number of times (given in parentheses) before it must be reloaded; reloading takes an action or bonus action (usually specified for the weapon).

###### **Rifled.**
Fitted with spiral grooves, this weapon spins its projectiles for superior stability and range. It does not suffer disadvantage when attacking at long range, its shots flying true where others would falter.

###### **Scoped.**
Fitted with a magnifying optic, this weapon allows precise shots at great distance. It extends its normal range to three‑quarters of its long range, giving the marksman a clearer sight picture. Additionally, it has disadvantage on attack rolls against targets within 20 feet

###### **Shotgun.**
Favored by close‑quarters fighters, this weapon is devastating at short range. When fired within half its normal range, it doubles its damage dice.

###### **Sniper.**
A weapon of exquisite precision, it finds the gaps in armor with unerring accuracy. This weapon scores a critical hit on a natural roll one better than normal (e.g., 19–20 instead of 20).

###### **Supercharge ().**
A weapon that can be pushed far beyond its normal limits, at great risk. Before firing, you may supercharge it: roll double damage dice, but suffer an overheat on natural attack rolls of what is listed in the parenthesis. If you overheat, you take the damage (including AP) on a failed Dexterity save, or half on a success.

###### **Tension.**
A bow or crossbow built with a heavy draw, this weapon can be loosed with either muscle or finesse. You may use either your Strength or Dexterity modifier for attack and damage rolls (you must use the same for both).

###### **Twin‑linked.**
Two weapons joined as one, firing in perfect synchrony. You have advantage on attack rolls (or the target has disadvantage on saves against effects) with this weapon, and you fire twice the number of shots. This requires that you wield both weapons at the same time.

# Armour Properties
###### **Adamantine (Armor).**
Reinforced with plates of the legendary dark metal, this armor is so resilient that even the most telling blows glance aside. Suits of armor reinforced with adamantine turn all critical hits against the wearer into normal hits, denying fate its cruelest strokes.

###### **Arcane.**
This armor is etched with elaborate sigils and runes, creating a reservoir of minor magic. It allows the wearer to cast two chosen cantrips while wearing it, with Intelligence as the spellcasting ability.

###### **Cleated.**
Fitted with sharp, angled ridges, this armor plants the wearer firmly against any force that would displace them. When you would be involuntarily moved, reduce that movement by 10 feet, the cleats digging into the ground.

###### **Climbing.**
This armor incorporates crampons, grapnels, and coiled rope, allowing the wearer to scale sheer surfaces. While you have a hand free, you gain a climb speed equal to your movement speed, turning vertical walls into mere obstacles.

###### **Cloaking.**
Woven with threads of shadow and illusion, this light armor allows the wearer to fade from sight. You can cast the _invisibility_ spell without consuming a spell slot; once used, it recharges after a short or long rest, the magic drawing from ambient energy.

###### **Clockwork.**
Fitted with intricate gears and springs that whir with every step, this heavy armor augments the wearer's strength and speed. It increases your movement speed by 10 feet, triples your jump distance, and grants advantage on Strength (Athletics) checks (except grappling), the mechanisms lending power to your limbs.

###### **Comfortable.**
Designed for the long march, this armor is padded and articulated to allow rest without removal. You may sleep in this armor without suffering any ill effects, waking as refreshed as if you had doffed it.

###### **Diving.**
Equipped with webbed fins, a sealed mask, and an air bladder that holds one hour of breathable gas, this armour is made for the depths. It grants a swim speed equal to your movement speed, lets you breathe underwater or in a vacuum, and makes you immune to breathable poisons while the bladder is active; it refills during a long rest, ready for your next plunge.

###### **Dragon scale.**
Forged from the hide of a dragon type creature, this armor carries the creature's defiance. It gives you advantage on saving throws against breath weapons, and you gain resistance to the damage type associated with the creature (chosen from black, blue, brass, bronze, copper, gold, green, red, silver, or white).

###### **Environmental.**
Sealed and insulated against the elements, this armor allows the wearer to withstand extremes that would kill lesser beings. You ignore the harmful effects of temperatures from –100°F to 300°F, walking through blizzards and firestorms alike.

###### **Exotic (Armor).**
A suit of unusual make this armor is rare and difficult to craft. It grants a +1 bonus to Armor Class, its design baffling common weapons. It requires a special proficiency to be worn.

###### **Feather Light.**
So airy and responsive is this armor that the wearer moves with the grace of the wind. While wearing this armor, you do not provoke opportunity attacks, slipping away from threats as if you weighed nothing.

###### **Furious.**
When the wearer's blood is up and their body is battered, this armor seems to tighten and harden. When you are at half your hit points or fewer, you gain resistance to bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing damage.

###### **Ghostly.**
Infused with ectoplasm and the essence of the border ethereal, this armor allows the wearer to step between worlds. As a bonus action, you may enter the [[Aether]] (as the _etherealness_ spell) for up to 10 minutes, which may be split across multiple uses; the armor regains all expended time on a long rest, the spectral energy replenishing.

###### **Golem.**
A suit of heavy armor of such immense proportions that the wearer seems to swell in size. You are treated as if under the "Enlarge" effect of the _enlarge/reduce_ spell—your bulk and reach magnified, your presence filling the battlefield.

###### **Hyper.**
This light armour is a masterpiece of speed, its components so fluid that they seem to anticipate the wearer's movements. Your movement increases by 10 feet, you have advantage on initiative rolls, and you may Dash as a bonus action, becoming a blur of motion.

###### **Immortal.**
This medium or heavy armor is suffused with life‑preserving energies. You have advantage on death saving throws and your hit point maximum is considered 50 higher for determining instant death from massive damage; rolling a 20 on a death save restores hit points equal to your level + your constitution modifier, as the armor itself fights to keep you alive.

###### **Integrated.**
This armour is fitted with concealed weapon housings—one per arm, and none for two‑handed weapons. When you draw an integrated weapon, it snaps into your hand and cannot be disarmed; when stowed, it retracts into the armor's plating. You may change which weapons are integrated during a long rest, tailoring your arsenal to the coming fight.

###### **Juggernaut.**
A fortress of steel, this heavy armour makes the wearer an immovable bulwark. You count as an obstacle providing three‑quarters cover (instead of half cover) to creatures within 5 feet—and you must have Strength 18 or higher to wear it proficiently, for its weight would crush a lesser warrior.

###### **Magnetic (Armor).**
Reinforced with lodestone and a humming electric coil, this medium or heavy armor can be activated as a bonus action. While active, any ranged attack against a creature within 5 feet of you targets you instead. You may deactivate it as a bonus action, releasing the field.

###### **Maneuvering.**
This light or medium armor fires spring‑loaded grappling hooks, granting unparalleled mobility. As a reaction when falling or as a bonus action on your turn, you can project a hook to a location within your movement speed; if it can bear your weight, you are pulled there without provoking opportunity attacks. Requires Dexterity 16 or higher.

###### **Mithril (Armor).**
Crafted from the legendary silver‑grey metal, this armor is incredibly light and flexible. It weighs half as much, has no Strength requirement, does not impose disadvantage on Stealth checks, and its maximum Dexterity bonus increases by 1; medium mithril armor can even be worn hidden under clothing..

###### **Overshield.**
This medium armor projects a shimmering field of force that absorbs harm. As a bonus action, you gain temporary hit points equal to half your level.

###### **Quick‑Change.**
Designed for rapid deployment, this armor uses clever straps and buckles that release with a single pull. You can don or doff this armor as an action, ready for battle in an instant.

###### **Resistance.**
Enchanted with elemental warding, this armor protects against a chosen damage type. While wearing this armor, you have resistance to that type.

###### **Retractable.**
One gauntlet of this medium or heavy armor contains a collapsible shield that extends with a snap. You can don or doff that shield as a bonus action, the shield springing from its housing like a metal flower.

###### **Spellguard.**
This armor is covered in protective sigils and arcane wards that create a barrier against enchantment and evocation. You have advantage on saving throws against spells, the runes flaring to life and unweaving magical energies.

###### **Spiked.**
A brutal suit of heavy armor bristling with iron spikes, it turns the wearer's body into a weapon. Any creature that is in contact with you (grappling, being grappled, or having swallowed you) takes piercing damage equal to 1d4 + your Strength modifier at the start of your turn, impaled by your very armour.

###### **Thermic.**
Fitted with heat‑dissipating channels and insulating layers, this armour is designed for the forge or the inferno. While wearing this armor, you have immunity to overheating and its effects, standing unscathed where others would be cooked alive.

###### **Trollskin.**
Lined with the regenerating hide of a troll, this armor lends its wearer some of that creature's legendary vitality. You may use a bonus action to regain 1d10 + your Constitution modifier hit points; you can do this twice, regaining all uses after a short or long rest, the flesh knitting itself anew.

###### **Winged.**
This light armor has a set of mechanical or magical wings that fold flat against the back until needed. You can extend them as a bonus action, granting you a fly speed equal to your movement speed.

# Shield Properties
###### **Bash.**
A shield with this property is weighted and edged for offense, transforming defense into a stunning counterblow. As a bonus action, make a melee attack to slam your shield into a creature; on a hit, the target becomes stunned until the start of your next turn. This deals damage equal to the shield's AC bonus—a crash of metal and bone that leaves foes reeling.

###### **Shield Parry.**
This shield is light and responsive, its balance perfect for deflecting incoming strikes with a flick of the wrist. As a reaction, add your proficiency bonus to your Armor Class against one incoming attack that you can see, turning aside a blow that would have found its mark.

###### **Shield Wall.**
Designed to lock together with other shields or form an unbreachable barrier, this shield can be planted to become a mobile fortress. As an action, deploy the shield; while deployed, you gain three-quarters cover against attacks from the front, but you are unable to move—rooted like a stone wall against the tide of battle.

###### **Banner Shield.**
Bearers of this shield are expected to lead from the front, its surface emblazoned with a standard that rallies allies. While you wield this shield and are not incapacitated, allies within 30 feet gain advantage on saving throws against fear effects, and you may plant the shield as an action to raise the banner high, inspiring allies within 60 feet as if under the effect of a _heroism_ spell (Concentration not required, and the effect persists as long as you remain within 10 feet of the planted shield). You may use this feature once per long rest.

###### **Board Shield.**
A broad, rectangular shield often used by sailors and boarding parties, its large surface is perfect for clearing space and controlling corridors. When you take the Attack action, you may forgo one attack to make a Shove attempt against a creature within 5 feet; you have advantage on the Strength (Athletics) check, and if you shove the creature 5 feet or more, you may immediately move into the space it vacated.

###### **Buckler.**
A diminutive shield strapped to the forearm, the buckler is the favored defense of duelists, rogues, and those who prefer a weapon in each hand. A buckler grants only a +1 bonus to Armor Class, but it counts as a light weapon for the purposes of two‑weapon fighting, and its compact size allows you to don or doff it as an object interaction. Additionally, you may still use a hand wearing a buckler to perform somatic components for spellcasting, for the buckler leaves your fingers free.

###### **Fortress Shield.**
An enormous pavise meant to be planted like a door, this shield is a portable wall. Deploying the Fortress Shield requires an action, and while deployed you gain total cover from the direction of deployment, though you are considered prone for the purpose of attacks from other angles. You may peek over or around the shield as a bonus action to make attacks without losing the cover, treating it as three‑quarters cover until the start of your next turn.

###### **Grappling Shield.**
This shield is fitted with hooks, barbs, and sharpened edges that can snag an opponent's weapon or limb. When you take the Attack action, you may forgo one attack to make a grapple attempt against a creature within 5 feet; you have advantage on the check, and while grappling with this shield the target cannot cast spells with somatic components or use two‑handed weapons, for the shield has them well and truly caught.

###### **Heater Shield.**
A classic, curved shield of moderate size, the heater is the most common shield on the battlefield, providing reliable defense without undue burden. It offers the standard +2 bonus to Armor Class and is compatible with all shield‑related feats and class features.

###### **Kite Shield.**
Tall and teardrop-shaped, the kite shield is crafted for mounted combat, its elongated form protecting both rider and steed. While mounted, a kite shield grants your mount an additional +1 bonus to its Armor Class, and you have advantage on saving throws to avoid being dismounted or unhorsed. Its shape also allows it to be planted like a pavise when fighting on foot, treating it as a standard shield with no additional benefit.

###### **Shield of Arrows.**
This shield is cut with narrow slits or is otherwise designed to catch and shatter projectiles. When you are hit by a ranged weapon attack, you may use your reaction to reduce the damage by an amount equal to the shield's AC bonus plus your Dexterity modifier, the shield deflecting or breaking the incoming missile.

###### **Spiked Shield.**
Bristling with iron spikes, this shield is as much a weapon as a defense. Its AC bonus remains standard (+2), but you may make an off‑hand attack with the spikes as a bonus action, dealing 1d4 piercing damage (plus your Strength modifier) on a hit.

###### **Targe.**
A small, round shield of unique design, the targe is lightweight and often carried with a single center‑grip, leaving the off‑hand free for a hand‑axe or seax. It grants +1 AC, and while wielding it you may draw or stow a one‑handed weapon as part of the same object interaction, its small size not interfering with your movements.

###### **Throwing Shield.**
Balanced for flight, this shield can be hurled like a discus to strike distant foes. As an action, you may make a ranged attack with the shield, using your Strength modifier and treating it as a thrown weapon with range (30/60). On a hit, it deals 1d6 bludgeoning damage and the target must make a Dexterity saving throw (DC = 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Strength modifier) or be knocked prone.

###### **Tower Shield.**
A massive, rectangular shield of immense height and weight, the tower shield is a walking wall. It grants a +3 bonus to Armor Class, but its bulk imposes disadvantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks, requires a Strength score of 15 or higher to wield effectively, and you have disadvantage on attack rolls made in the same turn you used your movement, for shifting the shield's weight is a labor.

###### **Warding Shield.**
This shield is carved with protective sigils and holy symbols that create a barrier against magic. While wielding this shield, you have advantage on saving throws against spells and magical effects, and you may use your reaction to grant a creature within 5 feet advantage on a saving throw against a spell—the ward extending to cover a comrade.
