# Overview
While most regard the Vessel as a fixed container, its shape having been set at birth, there exists a unique intersection of innate predisposition and cultivated mindset known as Abstraction. In these individuals, the Vessel is not something standard like a chalice or pot, but something more irregular. This gift arises because the Vessel itself influences its owner's very way of perception. Now, raw predisposition alone is not enough. To develop a true abstraction, one must adopt a mindset that both embraces their Vessel and their self in both full as one. Only through a union of talent and disciplined perception can an adept learn to mold their Vessel without falling into Dissonance. 

There remain two main types of abstraction, with one being seen as the first step to reaching the other: **Fixed Abstraction** and **Free Abstraction**. With fixed, the Vessel is still considered irregular as it is not a standard container; however, it remains a sense of rigidness in the way that the Vessel remains similar to that of a physical object still. Examples of this would be the missing leader of the former nation of [[Bezmadan]], [[Van K'vann]] (a powerful blacksmith having his Vessel abstract into a forge of its own), and [[Wuglo]], the founder of modern wizardry, who holds a Vessel in the form of a grimoire. 

Fixed Abstraction is generally considered the first true step towards understanding one's Vessel as an extension of the self. While its form may be unusual, it remains something that can be easily conceptualized by its bearer. A forge remains a forge, a grimoire remains a grimoire, and their respective functions provide a framework through which the bearer understands their own power. This rigidity is not inherently a weakness, however, as it allows the bearer to develop a much more stable relationship with their Vessel. Most who achieve Abstraction never progress beyond this state, either through lack of ability or because they find no reason to abandon the shape that they have come to understand. 

The distinction between the two forms is therefore not one of power, but of freedom. Fixed Abstraction gives the bearer an unusual Vessel that they can understand and manipulate according to its nature, while Free Abstraction allows the bearer to move beyond the need for a singular interpretation of what their Vessel must be. Where a fixed Vessel may be understood as a particular object, a free Vessel is instead understood through what it represents to its bearer. Its form may change, its internal structure may shift, and the manner in which is power manifests may differ depending upon the needs or understanding of its master.

This freedom, however, comes at a considerable cost. An adept of Free Abstraction must maintain an understanding of their Vessel even as that understanding changes. Without a rigid form to serve as a constant reference, the bearer must instead recognize the underlying identity that exists beneath its various manifestations. The Vessel may cease to resemble any physical object at all, becoming instead something defined by ideas, relationships, emotions, or other aspects of the self. It is for this reason that Free Abstraction is considered exponentially more difficult to master than its fixed counterpart. The more freedom one gives their Vessel, the more responsibility they bear for defining it.

Those who achieve this Free Abstraction are therefore not simply capable of more powerful expressions of their Vessel. They are also capable of expressing it without being bound to a singular interpretation. Their abilities may shift alongside their understanding of themselves, allowing techniques that would otherwise contradict the physical or conceptual limitations of a Fixed Abstraction. Such individuals are exceedingly rare, and even among those predisposed toward Abstraction, very few possess the discipline or self-awareness required to reach this state.

Those capable of Abstraction also tend to have an easier time achieving [[Synchronicity]], as an abstracted Vessel is less rigid than the traditional Vessel and can accommodate multiple forms of [[Essentias|Essentia]] more naturally. This is not an inherent property of Abstraction, however, and the two abilities remain independent of one another.

Abstraction is also closely related to [[Vessel Pour]] and [[Vessel Burst]], both of which represent advanced forms of self-expression through the Vessel. While these techniques require their own mastery, an understanding of one's Vessel is essential to either. Vessel Pour is exclusive to those who have achieved Abstraction, while Vessel Burst may be performed without it. 

Finally, Abstraction exists as an opposing principle to [[All Things Wretches|Wretching]]. Where the former requires an individual to understand and accept themselves as responsible for defining their Vessel, the latter represents the surrender of that responsibility. This distinction is particularly important when considering the nature of the Voice, though the exact relationship between the two remains poorly understood.
